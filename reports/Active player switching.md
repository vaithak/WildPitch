# Switch on events, not on proximity

Good football games keep switching calm with two separate decisions, and WildPitch should copy that split. One decision is **a "who should play the ball" oracle**. It scores team-mates by predicted intercept time and is held steady by hysteresis. The other is **a narrow gate on when the human's cursor may follow that oracle**: ball events (our pass struck, ball gone loose, a lofted ball, possession won or lost), plus a rare emergency override. Google Research Football (GRF) is the clearest open example. Its designated player changes only when a challenger's time ratio drops below 0.8 with a 500 ms offset, and the human's cursor follows him only on unique possession, at a set piece, or when he is **3x quicker** than the human's man. EA FC ships "On Air Balls and Loose Balls" as its auto-switch default in FIFA 22. It also adds three pieces that make a switch read as intended: a next-player indicator, momentum carry-over ("Move Assistance") and a reaction-time window on the switch button. WildPitch's Normal mode already scores loose balls by time with YSoccer's 0.5x band, so the owner's "just vicinity" belief is only partly right. Distance still drives the defender rule (D-029), the keeper hand-back and the manual Q button. The time it does use is not the earliest arrival and ignores the player's velocity and facing. Most cooldown-free triggers can chain, and an AI chaser chosen by `team_plan.c` keeps pulling the cursor away from a human whose stick is released. The redesign below changes three things. It replaces the per-rule cooldowns with one scorer, one dwell, a return lock and event windows. It suppresses switches while the stick shows intent. And it makes the manual button switch to exactly the man the indicator shows, cycling on repeated presses. All of it is integer-only and testable with per-reason switch counts. No switch rates have been measured yet, so every target below is a starting value, and Stage 0 measures the baseline.

## Commercial games treat switching as three separate problems

EA's settings menu is the most detailed public description of how a shipped game handles switching. It splits the topic into three problems: *when* the game may switch for you, *whom* a request picks, and *how the hand-over feels*. **Auto Switching** sets the "when". FIFA 22's official accessibility text describes it as deciding "how you will switch between players during certain passes" and lists Manual, Only on Loose Balls, Only on Air Balls, On Air Balls and Loose Balls, and Automatic, with the default "On Air Balls and Loose Balls" ([EA Accessibility, FIFA 22](https://www.ea.com/able/resources/fifa/fifa-22/ps5/customise-controls-settings)). In FC 25's wording, Auto means "AI will switch to the best player for you" and Manual means "AI will never switch for you" ([FIFAUTeam FC 25](https://fifauteam.com/fc-25-controller-settings/)). Guides disagree about the FC 27 default. FIFAUTeam marks Only on Air Balls ([FIFAUTeam FC 27](https://fifauteam.com/?p=337819)), while DTGRE says On Air Balls and Loose Balls ([DTGRE](https://www.dtgre.com/2026/09/ea-fc-27-player-switching-settings-wrong-defender.html)). Both readings are event-gated. In none of EA's defaults does the cursor follow proximity continuously.

The "how it feels" settings say the most about flicker. **Auto Switching Move Assistance** keeps "the player you AUTO switch to moving in his current direction for a short period of time. This will allow you to orient your intended direction for the new player." Its default was High in FIFA 22 ([EA Accessibility, FIFA 22](https://www.ea.com/able/resources/fifa/fifa-22/ps5/customise-controls-settings)) and None in FIFA 21 ([EA Accessibility, FIFA 21](https://www.ea.com/able/resources/fifa/fifa-21/xbox-one/controller-settings)). These are different years, not a contradiction, and the FC 27 guide again marks None ([FIFAUTeam FC 27](https://fifauteam.com/?p=337819)). **Reaction Time Calibration** is newer and more revealing. It switches "you to the player that was indicated when you initially thought about switching". DTGRE says it "can ignore the currently recommended player and switch to the previous recommendation instead", with a tunable window ([FIFAUTeam FC 27](https://fifauteam.com/?p=337819); [DTGRE](https://www.dtgre.com/2026/09/ea-fc-27-player-switching-settings-wrong-defender.html)). A game only needs that feature if its own next-player recommendation changes often enough to cause mis-switches. That is indirect evidence that EA's L1 target flickers, and that EA chose to absorb the flicker at the moment of the press rather than suppress it at the source.

For the "whom", EA shows a **Next Player Switch Indicator**: "shows which player you'll swap controls with when pressing L1 … while defending", on by default ([EA Accessibility, FIFA 22](https://www.ea.com/able/resources/fifa/fifa-22/ps5/customise-controls-settings)). Since FC 25 the L1 rule itself can be chosen: Classic ("weighs several factors"), Closest to Ball, or Goal Side, which "favors players between the ball and your goal" ([FIFAUTeam FC 27](https://fifauteam.com/?p=337819)). A right-stick flick picks a player in a direction, measured from your player (Player Relative, the default) or from the ball. A 0-10 sensitivity weighs the flick direction against proximity. Adaptive reaches further the longer the stick is held. Player Rotation moves the indicator and leaves L1 to confirm, a preview-then-commit interaction ([FIFAUTeam FC 27](https://fifauteam.com/?p=337819); [DTGRE](https://www.dtgre.com/2026/09/ea-fc-27-player-switching-settings-wrong-defender.html)). The smaller GOALS game ships the same vocabulary, cut down to an auto-switch of "Air Balls" or "Off" plus an indicator that "shows you exactly who you'll switch to next" ([GOALS settings guide](https://playgoals.com/en/gamesettings-guide)).

Konami's history shows the trade-off between frequent and calm switching most directly. PES 2019's manual describes L1 as moving "the cursor onto a player who is close to the ball" and the right stick as pointing "towards the direction of the player you wish to take control of" ([Konami PES 2019 manual](https://dds.konami.com/games/manual/pes2019/PC/en-us/control_other.html)). One PES 2019 player reported that Assisted changed the cursor essentially only when the opponent passed, so a dribbler running at goal left the cursor on a distant defender ([Evoweb](https://evoweb.uk/threads/closest-player-switching-flaw-in-pes-2019.79328/)). eFootball 2023 v2.2.0 then made Assisted switch "to a player who is near the ball and more suited to the flow of the play" and "in a more frequent manner". The old behaviour was kept as a new "Semi-Auto" option, and existing Assisted users were moved onto it ([Konami v2.2.0 patch notes](https://www.konami.com/efootball/en/page/2023/season2_patch-notes_v2-20)). Konami changed the default and preserved the old behaviour, which reads as an admission that players genuinely split on how often they want switching. Recent EA work points the same way. FC 27 lets "a manually-controlled defender … reach further than an AI-controlled defender" and asks you to switch "to the player actively defending" ([EA FC 27 deep dive](https://www.ea.com/games/ea-sports-fc/fc-27/news/pitch-notes-fc27-gameplay-deep-dive)), and its v1.2 notes list "Loose Ball Switching Improvements" ([EA FC 27 v1.2](https://www.ea.com/games/ea-sports-fc/fc-27/news/update-v1-2-pitch-notes)). Both raise the cost of a bad switch. Two games show where the design space ends. Mario Strikers switches automatically to the nearest character or the ball holder ([Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/2022/06/mario-strikers-battle-league-how-to-switch-characters/)). Rematch removes switching entirely, because each human is one footballer ([Rematch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rematch_%28video_game%29)). No publisher documents a dwell time, a neutral-stick rule or a scoring formula. Everything numeric below comes from open code.

## Open engines rank by intercept time and rarely override the human

**GRF scores players by when they can reach the predicted ball, not by where it is now.** Each player steps through the ball prediction, considers only points below 1.5 m, and takes the first future time at which his run time, computed from his position *and current movement*, is no later than the ball. The search uses coarse steps first and then refines in 10 ms steps. The passer or shooter is excluded for the first 500 ms after his kick, so he cannot win the ball straight back from his own receiver ([GRF player.cpp](https://github.com/google-research/football/blob/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/player/player.cpp)). A team-level "designated possession player" takes the argmin of that time, guarded by stacked hysteresis. A challenger replaces the incumbent only if `(t_best+500)/(t_cur+500) < 0.8`. Possession multiplies the rating by 0.5, being human-controlled multiplies it by 0.8 (both in the incumbent's favour), and the rating is pushed up by `(r+0.2)·1.2` when the incumbent already beats the nearest opponent by 100 ms. The source comment states the goal as "switch only if other player is somewhat better, to overcome possession-chaos" ([GRF team.cpp](https://github.com/google-research/football/blob/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/team.cpp)). The +500 ms offset carries most of the weight. Near the ball, where times are small, it turns a ratio test into an absolute margin of roughly 100 ms or more. That is exactly where a pure ratio flips on noise.

**GRF's human cursor follows that oracle only in narrow cases.** `Team::UpdateSwitch` selects the designated player when he has unique possession, during a set piece, or when `3 * t_designated < t_human`, and never selects the goalkeeper. A manual press goes to the designated player, then the best possession player, then a positional target. It is refused while the human's man has unique possession ([GRF team.cpp](https://github.com/google-research/football/blob/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/team.cpp)). The positional target is `AI_GetBestSwitchTargetPlayer`. When defending it prefers the nearest of eight candidates whose look-ahead position is goal-side. It receives the stick direction as a parameter but never uses it ([GRF AIfunctions.cpp](https://github.com/google-research/football/blob/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/AIsupport/AIfunctions.cpp)). After any switch, GRF blends the player's current movement into the stick input for 300 ms. `GetLastSwitchBias()` falls linearly from 1 to 0, and the blend is `current·bias^0.7·0.5 + manual·(1−bias^0.7·0.5)`, so at most half of the old motion survives ([GRF humancontroller.cpp](https://github.com/google-research/football/blob/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/player/controller/humancontroller.cpp); [GRF playercontroller.cpp](https://github.com/google-research/football/blob/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/player/controller/playercontroller.cpp)). This is EA's Move Assistance in code.

YSoccer, which WildPitch already borrowed from in D-037, adds two defensive refinements. With a loose ball it switches to `near1` only if his "frames to reach" from a 2-second ball prediction is below **0.5x** the controlled man's. It refuses while the controlled man is still kicking ("avoid stealing controls while still kicking") and refuses when the candidate is the passing mate ("passing mate, let him get the ball"). When the opponent owns the ball it switches to a **best defender**: the player closest to the ball among those nearer their own goal than 0.95x the ball's distance to that goal. The best defender has his own hysteresis, being replaced only by a challenger under 0.9x his distance ([YSoccer Team.java](https://sourceforge.net/p/ysoccer/code/ci/master/tree/java/core/src/main/java/com/ygames/ysoccer/match/Team.java)). SWOS is the cautionary baseline. It switches every frame to the man nearest the ball by squared pixel distance, with a **25-tick cooldown** only after a pass receiver takes control ([SWOS swos.asm](https://github.com/zlatkok/swos-port/blob/master/swos/swos.asm); [SWOS updatePlayers.cpp](https://github.com/zlatkok/swos-port/blob/master/src/game/updatePlayers/updatePlayers.cpp)). One indie devlog descended from Sensible Soccer shows a directional alternative. A stick pointing left selects "the closest player to the _right_ of the ball (within a certain cone)", the man who would run toward the ball in that direction, and the author says it "really helps when the action gets frenetic" ([punyrobot devlog](https://punyrobot.itch.io/superliquidsoccer/devlog/352077/new-player-selection-scheme)).

| Engine | Score | Hysteresis | When the human's cursor moves | Post-switch |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SWOS | squared distance to ball now | none (25-tick timer after receiver hand-over) | every frame, nearest man | none |
| YSoccer | frames to reach, 2 s prediction | challenger < 0.5x; defender < 0.9x distance | loose ball (0.5x), opponent ball (best goal-side defender), own owner | none |
| GRF | earliest intercept from position + movement, 500 ms passer exclusion | (t+500)/(t'+500) < 0.8, x0.5 possession, x0.8 human, beat-the-opponent bonus | unique possession, set piece, or 3x quicker; manual press | 300 ms blend, ≤ 50% old motion |
| EA FC (settings only) | not published ("several factors", Goal Side, Closest) | Reaction Time Calibration on the L1 target | air and loose balls by default; L1; right-stick flick | Move Assistance None…High |
| eFootball | "near the ball … flow of play" | not published | Assisted (frequent) vs Semi-Auto (old, calmer) | not published |

Three lessons transfer. First, score by intercept time from position *and* velocity, so two runners crossing paths do not swap ranks at the moment their distances cross. Second, put hysteresis on the oracle itself, not only on the cursor, so the indicator is stable enough to show. Third, let the human's cursor move without being asked only on events, or when another man is overwhelmingly better placed.

## WildPitch switches on eight triggers with no common dwell

All switching lives in `src/sim/seats.c`, and the code audit (HEAD 570b31c) counts **eight triggers**. Four of them switch immediately with no cooldown check: the own-pass hand-off on the contact tick (`seats_pass_handoff`), the deflection switch (`deflect_switch`), the keeper hand-back, and `seats_on_gain`, which fires when an AI team-mate, our keeper or a restart taker wins the ball. The manual SWITCH press also ignores the cooldown, though it then locks auto-switching for 90 ticks. Only the remaining rules read `switch_cool`: the D-029 "they have it" rule (45 ticks after it fires) and the loose-ball rules (30 ticks). **No minimum dwell applies across triggers**, so a gain, a loss and a loose ball can produce a switch roughly every 0.5 s, with the cooldown-free switches landing between them. In Normal, the shipped default, the loose-ball rule uses time with YSoccer's 0.5x band (`T_SWITCH_TIME_K`). In a dead heat it is stable, because neither man is under half the other's time. But its time is `meet_choose`'s `m.t`: the ball's arrival at the reachable point *closest to the player*, not his earliest arrival. It ignores his velocity and facing, so a man sprinting away scores the same as one running at the ball. When the current man has no reachable point within the 4 s horizon, his time jumps to the 9.0 s fallback, and any team-mate who has one wins in a single tick.

The other rules are distance rules, which is where the owner's intuition is right. **D-029** switches to the team-mate nearest a spot 1.5 m "goal-side" of the ball, offset only along x, whenever he is more than 3 m nearer than the current man. It ignores the ball's y, every player's velocity and whether anyone is actually goal-side. It also applies while the opponent's pass is travelling, so the spot moves at pass speed. The audit's reasoned (not measured) ceiling is **one switch every 45 ticks, about 1.3 per second**, while a carrier dribbles from one defender toward another. The keeper hand-back goes to whoever is nearest the ball at the moment of release, often the man next to the keeper rather than the target. The manual Q picks the nearest man other than the current one, so repeated presses **ping-pong between the two nearest men** and never reach a third. `team_plan.c` makes all of this worse. A seat's man counts as the ball-winner only if he is fastest *and* engaging (within 4 m, or moving at the ball). Otherwise an AI team-mate is assigned to chase. With the stick released, the human's man is not engaging. So the AI mate B runs in, the 0.5x rule hands B to the human, B (now human, stick released) stops, the old man A becomes the AI winner, and A can take control back on gain. That is a plausible **A→B→A within 1-2 s with no human input**, and it matches the owner's "frequent switching … when a couple of players near the ball" more closely than any distance effect does.

Presentation amplifies every switch. The only marker is a yellow triangle over the controlled man in `render.c`, with no next-man preview, no transition cue, and a camera target 60% on the seat's player, so each switch also moves the camera. Test coverage has a gap in the same place. `test_passing.c` runs with assist Off, so it exercises the legacy distance rule. The scripted determinism match's "750 switches" figure is dominated by random SWITCH presses on 5v5 with assist Off. **No test asserts the shipped Normal time rule or any anti-flicker property.** One documentation mismatch matters for the redesign. D-037 says the time switch is "no longer blocked by a pass to the other team", but in code an opponent pass in flight sets `theirs`, and the D-029 distance branch handles the tick and `continue`s before the time rule runs.

## Redesign: one scorer, event gates, a visible next man

The redesign keeps every owner decision as a trigger: hand-off on contact (D-017), deflection (D-020), control jumping to the AI mate or keeper who wins the ball (D-022) and the defender auto-switch (D-029). What changes is how each trigger picks a man and when it may fire. **Assist Off stays the legacy sim bit for bit**, as D-037 promised, so the 5v5 golden and `test_passing` keep their meaning. Low, Normal and High get the new behaviour. The redesign amends D-017, D-020, D-029 and D-037 and should be recorded as one new decision. D-047 is already referenced by in-progress aerial code, so the next free number is probably D-048. Every switch records a reason, which every acceptance metric is scoped by. The reasons are `SW_PASS`, `SW_GAIN`, `SW_DEFLECT`, `SW_KEEPER`, `SW_MANUAL`, `SW_MANUAL_DIR`, `SW_EVENT`, `SW_OVERRIDE` and `SW_KICKOFF`. The first four are **forced** (ball events or owner rules that must fire at once). `SW_EVENT` and `SW_OVERRIDE` are **unprompted**, and only these are gated.

### Score: earliest intercept in ticks, with momentum and facing

A new `switch_eta(s, j, path)` replaces `meet_choose` for switching only. `meet_choose` also drives the AI chase, the team plan and the D-020 receive, so editing it would change the AI. The ball path is predicted **once per tick** for each team that has a seat, into a stack array: 60 steps of 3 ticks (3 s, GRF's horizon), with the bounce law `meet_chase` already copies from `ball.c`, and no allocation. For each candidate j and step k at ball time `T_k = 3k` ticks with height `z_k ≤ WP_BAND_BODY_MAX` (1.45 m), his arrival time at point `P_k` is built from WildPitch's own movement model in four terms. The **look-ahead start** is `p0 = pos + vel·T_SW_LOOK` with 0.2 s, GRF's extrapolation, so a man running away starts further away and a man running in starts nearer. The **turn cost** is `t_turn = T_SW_TURN_S · (1 − cos(facing, P_k − pos)) / 2`; a full reversal costs 0.30 s, consistent with `T_TURN_DEG_S_SPRINT` = 420°/s (180° in 0.43 s, overlapping the reaction time). The **acceleration cost** is `t_acc = T_SW_ACC_PEN · (1 − clamp(v_along/top, 0, 1))`, where `T_SW_ACC_PEN = top/(2·T_ACCEL)` ≈ 8/36 ≈ 0.22 s, the time a standing man loses reaching sprint speed at 18 m/s². **Reachability** follows: he can play it at step k if `T_CONTACT_REACT + t_turn + t_acc + |P_k − p0| / top ≤ T_k`.

The check stays exactly integer by squaring both sides. With `slack = T_k − react − turn − acc` (fx, seconds), the test is `fv2_len2_wide(P_k − p0) ≤ (int64)fx_mul(top, slack)²` when `slack > 0`, using int64 squares of Q16.16 values. The turn and acceleration terms still need the unit bearing to `P_k`, which costs one `fv2_len` per step. To skip it, compute both terms once per player against the bearing to the path's midpoint (step 30) and accept a slightly cruder facing term; `bench_sim` should decide which. The ETA is the first k that passes, converted to ticks (`uint16`). If no step passes, the ETA continues smoothly: `180 + ticks(react + turn + acc + |P_end − p0|/top)`, computed once with `fv2_len`. **That removes the 9.0 s cliff.** When an opponent owns the ball, the target becomes a **press point**: the carrier's position led by `vel·T_SW_DEF_LEAD` (0.3 s), plus `T_SW_GS_DIST` (1.5 m) along the unit vector from the ball to *our goal centre*. This is the same geometry `team_plan.c` already uses for its press point, so it is no longer an x-only offset. A man already beaten, with `dot(pos − ball, u_goal) < −T_SW_GS_TOL`, pays `T_SW_BEATEN_PEN`. That borrows YSoccer's goal-side test and EA's Goal Side rule. An opponent pass in flight is scored as a loose ball on the shared path, so control follows the predicted interception point, not the ball's current spot. Cost per tick is one 60-step prediction plus at most 7 × 60 multiply-compares, and up to 420 `fv2_len` calls if the per-step bearing is kept. That should be cheaper than today's seven 80-step `meet_choose` runs, but `bench_sim` must confirm it.

### Gate: forced triggers, event windows, a 3x override, a dwell and a return lock

Each seat keeps an **indicator candidate** `sw_next[k]`, the best eligible team-mate (outfield, not another seat's man, not the seat's own man). Its hysteresis follows GRF: the incumbent is kept unless `(t_c + K)·5 < (t_inc + K)·4`, with `K = T_SW_K` = 30 ticks. This is the 0.8 ratio with a 0.5 s offset, compared in `int32` with no division (the largest term is (65535+30)·5 < 2³¹). The incumbent is also dropped when he becomes ineligible. Every comparison below has the same cross-multiplied form, `(t_c + K)·DEN < (t_me + K)·NUM`.

Forced triggers fire as they do today, with better targets. The hand-off still switches on the contact tick. `seats_on_gain` still makes control jump to the AI mate or keeper who wins the ball, which preserves D-022's `bad_owner == 0` invariant. Deflection and the keeper hand-back now pick by `switch_eta` (the keeper's argmin includes the pass target if one was set), instead of `meet_choose`'s `m.t` or distance. Forced switches ignore the dwell but set it.

An unprompted switch needs four things at once. The **dwell** must have run out: `sw_dwell[k] == 0`, where every switch sets `sw_dwell` to `T_SW_DWELL` = 24 ticks (0.4 s), and a manual one sets it to `T_SW_LOCK_MANUAL` = 90. There must be **no other block**: phase is PLAY, the seat's man does not own the ball, he is not busy (kick, header or **a held kick charge**, so charges are no longer silently discarded), he is not the pass target, and no pass to another team-mate is in flight. The **return lock** must not apply: the candidate is not the man this seat left by an auto-switch within the last `T_SW_BACK` = 60 ticks (`sw_prev`, `sw_back`). And one of the two **ratio rules** below must fire.

The ratio depends on the state of play. The **defensive ratio** `NUM_E/DEN_E` (0.6 on Normal) applies inside an **event window** and for as long as **the opponent owns the ball or has a pass in flight**. An event window, `sw_event[k] = T_SW_EVENT_WIN` (9 ticks), opens when the ball goes loose by any route other than our own targeted pass, or when our pass ends without a receiver. Holding the defensive ratio for the whole opponent possession is what keeps the D-029 owner request alive against a carrier who never passes, the PES 2019 failure cited above. It also keeps `test_ai_rules` #6 passing, where the opponent starts on the ball with no event. There, a defender at about 58 ticks against a striker at about 162 ticks satisfies `(58+30)·5 = 440 < (162+30)·3 = 576`. On **our own or a loose ball outside a window**, only the **override** applies, with the GRF-style margin `NUM_O/DEN_O` (1/3 on Normal). **Stick intent** raises the bar but never removes it. Intent means the stick is held at or above `T_CHASE_STICK_MIN` (48) and points within `T_SW_INTENT_COS` (cos 50°) of his ETA point or the ball. Under intent, either rule needs a 4x margin (1/4) on Low and Normal and 3x on High. A human chasing a ball he can plausibly reach keeps his man, while one hopelessly beaten by a dribbler still gets switched.

Two last pieces close the A→B→A loop in `team_plan.c`. First, the "fastest AND engaging" gate gets GRF's human-incumbent bonus. The seat's man counts as winner when `(t_h + 6)·5 ≤ (t_ai + 6)·6` in ticks (within 1.2x, with a 0.1 s offset), or when he is engaging. Second, for as long as the return lock runs (`T_SW_BACK`, 60 ticks), the man the seat just left is excluded from the AI winner pick. So the old man cannot sprint back in and win the ball inside the ping-pong window, which would trigger an on-gain switch straight back.

```c
/* seats.c — unprompted gate, assist != OFF (sketch; integer only, int32 products) */
uint16_t tme = switch_eta(s, me, &path), tc = switch_eta(s, cand, &path);
int intent = stick_mag2(si) >= T_CHASE_STICK_MIN*T_CHASE_STICK_MIN && stick_cos(si, eta_pt_me, b->pos) >= T_SW_INTENT_COS;
int defend = s->sw_event[k] || they_have_it(s, p->team);            /* owner or pass in flight */
int32_t num = defend ? SW_EV_NUM[lv] : SW_OV_NUM[lv], den = defend ? SW_EV_DEN[lv] : SW_OV_DEN[lv];
if (intent) { num = 1; den = SW_INTENT_X[lv]; }                      /* 4, 4, 3 */
int32_t a = (int32_t)tc + T_SW_K, m = (int32_t)tme + T_SW_K;
if (a * den < m * num && !s->sw_dwell[k] && !(cand == s->sw_prev[k] && s->sw_back[k]))
    do_switch(s, k, cand, T_SW_DWELL, si, 1, s->sw_event[k] || defend ? SW_EVENT : SW_OVERRIDE);
```

### Manual: the press goes where the indicator points, and repeats cycle

A SWITCH press goes to **exactly `sw_next[k]`**, the man the indicator shows, so the press is predictable. It fires **on the press edge**, as today, so it adds no latency. To borrow EA's Reaction Time Calibration, if `sw_next` changed within the last `T_SW_RTC` = 6 ticks, the press goes to the previous candidate (`sw_next_old`). The oracle's hysteresis should make this rare. **Repeated taps cycle.** Within `T_SW_CYCLE_WIN` (40 ticks) of the last manual switch, a tap goes to the best-ETA eligible man not yet visited in this chain, using a `uint16` visited mask that includes the starting man. When everyone has been visited, the mask resets. Three quick taps in 7v7 reach three different men instead of toggling between two.

A **directional switch** has to work on a keyboard, where the left stick is always busy moving the player. The proposal is EA's Player Rotation adapted. The press has already switched to the indicated man. If SWITCH is still **held** after `T_SW_HOLD` (8 ticks), aim mode starts: that man's legs read a released stick, so he coasts to a stop, and the stick moves the indicator instead. The indicator is measured **ball-relative**, which suits a camera that is 40% ball and is the devlog's frenetic-play scheme. Releasing SWITCH commits a second switch (`SW_MANUAL_DIR`) if the aimed man differs. A tap released before 8 ticks is just the plain press. The selection is integer-only. Take each eligible mate q with `v = q − ref`, keep those where `dot(dir, v) ≥ T_SW_DIR_COS·|v|` (cos 45°), and minimise `|v|·(2 − cos)`, using `fv2_len` once per mate on a press, not per tick. A **gamepad right-stick flick** would be nicer but is not free. `WpSeatInput` has no second stick, R3 is bound to zoom, and adding `aim_x/aim_y` changes the input struct that replays and tests record. It is **the owner's call** and a v1.1 item.

### Indicator, transition cue and post-switch blend

`sw_next[k]` is sim state, hashed and covered by the `sim_hash()` size assert like `pick_recv`. The new `WP_SIMSTATE_SIZE_EXPECTED` should be computed at implementation time, since `sim.h` currently has uncommitted aerial edits. `render.c` draws it as a small hollow chevron in the seat's colour, dimmer than the yellow triangle, whenever our team does not own the ball and no pass of ours is in flight. That is EA's "while defending", extended to loose balls. The marker turns solid in the 9-tick event window and while the override is within 1.25x of firing, so an automatic switch is telegraphed and **arrives on a man who was already marked**. That makes a switch feel confirmed rather than surprising. The presentation layer can also add a 10-frame trail from the old man to the new one, derived from `seat_player` changing between frames, with no sim write.

**Post-switch blend.** For `T_SW_BLEND` = 18 ticks (0.3 s, GRF) after any switch, **while the stick is held**, the new man's legs take `mv = vel_dir·|stick|·w + stick·(1 − w)`, with `w = (r·FX_ONE/18)/2` for r ticks remaining. That is linear and at most 50% old motion, GRF's cap without the 0.7 power. It targets the real artefact, a held stick meant for the old man that would otherwise yank the new one round at `T_ACCEL` = 18 m/s². It applies only when the D-020 receive and the existing latch chase are not driving the legs, which covers the case where the latch passes a stale stick raw while an opponent owns the ball. A released stick needs no blend, because `move()` already brakes at `T_STOP_DECEL` = 14 m/s², and a sprinter takes about 0.57 s to stop, longer than the blend itself. The latch stays as it is (jitter-tolerant, ≤ 30 ticks) for a held stick on a loose ball.

## Conclusion

The flicker the owner sees is less a bad metric than too many doors. Eight triggers, four of them without a cooldown, a distance rule for defenders, and an AI planner that does not know the cursor just left a man together let the cursor move whenever *some* rule agrees. The industry answer is to make the cursor deliberately hard to move without being asked and to show the human what the game is thinking. The answer is not a cleverer argmin. Once WildPitch has a stable, visible next-man oracle, the same object serves auto-switching, the manual button, the indicator and, later, a learned brain's "who should play this" decision, which is where D-008's utility scoring wants it.

Three questions are genuinely the owner's. Should Normal also allow the 3x override on loose balls, or should it be EA-style events-only, with the override kept for High? An "Auto switching: Manual / Events / Assisted" setting would mirror Konami's split. Is hold-to-aim worth one more verb on the keyboard, and should a right-stick input be added for gamepads? And should the manual lock stay at 1.5 s now that the dwell and return lock exist? The thresholds above are derived from open engines and WildPitch's own movement constants, not copied from any commercial game, which publishes none. Stage 0's baseline is what turns them from reasoned numbers into measured ones.
