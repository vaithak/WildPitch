# Plan every aerial touch before it arrives

WildPitch should handle aerial receptions with one deterministic pipeline. **The button records an intent, a per-seat buffer holds it, and a contact planner reads the ball's predicted height at the earliest reachable contact tick to choose the body part.** The physics of that body part then sets the outgoing ball. Choosing the body part from height at the predicted contact is common to every engine whose code was read (Google Research Football, swos-port, YSoccer) and to the documented controls of FIFA/FC and eFootball. GRF adds a buffered intent queue with a fallback to control touches, and that queue is what makes chains like chest-then-pass fall out for free.

The lob meter landed while this research ran (D-045: tap = auto lob, hold = 12–45 m along the aim, 4 m + 12% snap, flight-time lead). Its shape matches the FIFA/GRF convention that **hold means distance**, but three defects remain. The 9.5 m/s vz clamp turns every meter lob beyond about 25 m into a flat 15–24° drive at 23–36 m/s, and at 45 m the solver hits its 35 m/s search cap (own prototype of the sim's air model). The snap compares the landing spot with team-mates' *current* positions, so a sprinting runner 1.8 s from the drop is 10+ m from where the meter is aimed. And AI lobs still bypass the meter. The stance physics fits a single "speed budget" rule. A clean cushion returns the ball at about 1.6–1.9 m/s (effective restitution ≈ 0.1–0.2), and the ball reaches the feet 0.3–0.7 s later. A heading contact follows the measured impulse law v_out ≈ 0.63·u + 1.63·v_head. A volley is a ground strike with roughly 2× the error. All of it maps to a few fixed-point constants per stance. The work is a generalisation of the existing `header.c`, not a parallel system, and it ships in five playtestable stages that start with a small meter fix.

Basis tags used below: **[S]** cited source, **[C]** WildPitch code (repo `path:line`, from the audit note), **[P]** my own float prototype of WildPitch's air model (`T_AIR_DRAG` 0.013, substep 0.08 m, 60 Hz), **[D]** a design value or estimate to tune in playtest. None of the cushioned-restitution values, setup times or band edges below come from a measurement. No study measuring chest, thigh or cushioned-foot rebound was found, so those are physics-derived proposals.

## The new lob meter has the right shape but drives long balls

The audit note captured the lob before D-045, as an instant, edge-triggered kick. It aimed at "where he'll be in 0.8 s" with vz = 4 + 0.22·d clamped to 5–9.5 m/s [C audit, `src/sim/player.c:273-286`; D-019]. The meter committed during this research (`d91d041`, D-045) changed the trigger. LOB now joins the shared hold/release loop. A release within `T_LOB_TAP_TICKS` (12) is the old auto lob to the ringed mate. A longer hold gives h ∈ (0,1], and the ball lands `lob_meter_dist(h)` = 12–45 m along the aim. A team-mate within 4 m + 12% of that spot gets the ball, led onto his run. Targeted lobs now lead by an estimated flight time of 0.92·2·vz/g, iterated three times. The HUD shows a violet bar [C `src/sim/player.c:123-136, 296-316, 440-458`, `src/sim/tuning.h:190-197`, `src/game/render.c:267-277`; `docs/DECISIONS.md` D-045]. Its own measurement shows a consistent ~7% overshoot: meter distances of 17.5, 25.7 and 34.0 m land at 18.7, 27.5 and 36.2 m.

This matches the commercial convention well. The defects are in what it inherited. `lob_velocity` still uses the D-019 vz formula with the 9.5 m/s clamp. The horizontal speed comes from `ball_lob_speed_for`, a 16-step bisection over `ball_carry_distance`, which mirrors the integrator's air branch and searches vh up to 35 m/s [C `src/sim/ball.c:247-276`]. Running WildPitch's own air model on that formula (drag 0.013, substep 0.08 m, 60 Hz) shows what the clamp does over the meter's range [P]:

| Meter distance d | vz | Launch speed | Launch angle | Flight | Apex | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 12 m | 6.6 | 12.0 m/s | 34° | 1.31 s | 2.1 m | lob |
| 20 m | 8.4 | 16.5 | 31° | 1.62 | 3.2 | lob |
| 25 m | 9.5 (clamp) | 19.1 | 30° | 1.81 | 4.0 | clamp starts |
| 35 m | 9.5 | 27.1 | 21° | 1.76 | 3.8 | driven |
| 45 m (full meter) | 9.5 | 36.3 (vh at the 35 m/s search cap) | 15° | 1.71 | 3.6 | a laser, at the limit |

On the 80 × 52 m 7v7 pitch [C `src/sim/sim.c:13-15`], long switches are exactly the passes people lob. The top third of the meter currently produces a 15–21° drive, faster than a maximal instep kick (24.7 ± 2.5 m/s, [Dörge et al. 2002](https://people.stfx.ca/smackenz/Courses/HK474/Midterm/Dorge%202002%20Biomechanical%20differences%20in%20soccer%20kicking%20with%20the%20preferred%20and%20non-preferred%20leg.pdf)).

The meter has three smaller faults. **The snap uses current positions**: it tests |players[j].pos − land| [C `src/sim/player.c:131-135`], and a mate sprinting at 7 m/s during a 1.8 s flight ends up about 12 m from there, so aiming the meter at a runner's destination finds nobody or the wrong man. **The range is fixed** at 12–45 m on every pitch size, and the untargeted landing is not clamped inside the pitch. **Several paths skip the new logic**: the AI still lobs and crosses through the tap path (`k.power = 0`, "lob: no meter") [C `src/sim/brain.c:549, 662`], the handoff to the receiver's assist still passes his start position and a default time rather than the solved drop point [C `src/sim/player.c:396`], and the avatar still poses every lob as a chip at power 0.5 [C `src/game/render.c:239-242`].

Two overlaps on the LOB key are already resolved in code: the slide tackle wins the press edge when an opponent owns the ball within 3 m (D-043), and the keeper/throw-in branch consumes LOB first. A third is still open: LOB does not start a header, only PASS and SHOOT do [C `src/sim/player.c:436-439`].

Reception is the larger gap. There are three height bands, `WP_BAND_FEET_MAX` 0.55, `WP_BAND_BODY_MAX` 1.45 and `WP_BAND_HEAD_MAX` 2.05 m [C `src/sim/sim.h:75-79`]. Nothing reads `WP_BAND_HEAD_MAX`; header reach uses `header.c`'s private `HEAD_Z 1.70 ± 0.32` [C `src/sim/header.c:7-10`]. Any loose ball at or below 1.45 m that passes within 1.0 m (stretch 1.3 m) at under 16 m/s relative speed is "gained" by `gain_apply`. That function applies one spill formula to every height, and above the feet band it simply sets vz = 0 so the ball falls from wherever it is [C `src/sim/player.c:600-665`]. The relative-speed test is **2-D, so a steep lob's 5–13 m/s of vertical speed is never considered**. A ball between 1.45 and 1.80 m that nobody heads bounces off the body cylinder at restitution 0.3 [C `src/sim/ball.c:90-117`]. `meet_choose`, meanwhile, steers a lob's receiver to points up to 1.9 m high, which can be exactly that uncontrollable zone [C `src/sim/seats.c:53-90`]. There is no volley at all: a non-owner cannot kick a ball above 0.55 m [C `src/sim/player.c:31-38`]. Chest-then-pass works only by accident. The owner of a chest-controlled ball can strike it from 1 m up with the ground-pass solver, which assumes a rolling ball, so pace and meeting point are off [C `src/sim/player.c:307-366`]. A PASS tap released before the ball arrives is silently dropped, and a PASS held through the reception turns into a through ball [C `src/sim/player.c:416-432`]. Header passes do exist, but they aim at the mate's *current* position with a fixed vz of 1.8 and speed 0.7·d + 3, hard-coded outside `tuning.h` [C `src/sim/header.c:98-134`].

## Engines let the button pick intent and the ball pick the body

The commercial convention for lofted passes is consistent. In FIFA/FC, **hold time on the lob button sets power, which players read as distance**, and modifiers choose the trajectory class rather than the meter: High Lob is L1+□, Driven Lob R1+□ in FIFA 20 and L1+R1+□ in FC 26/27, Flair Lob L2+□, and a double tap gives a low cross ([fifauteam FIFA 20 manual](https://fifauteam.com/fifa-21-manual/); [fifplay FC 27](https://www.fifplay.com/fc-27-lob-pass/); [Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/fifa/the-ultimate-fifa-21-passing-crossing-gameplay-guide-1407583)). Under assisted passing, the power mostly selects *which* team-mate receives the ball. EA's own mobile documentation calls lob passes "fully assisted" and says driven lobs add receiver trap error "based on distance and speed combined with the receiver's Ball Control" ([EA Advanced Passing](https://www.ea.com/able/news/gameplay-advanced-passing)). FC 26 now flattens lobs automatically "when the path is unobstructed" and makes crosses "lead more in front of the target" ([EA FC 26 deep dive](https://www.ea.com/games/ea-sports-fc/fc-26/news/pitch-notes-fc26-gameplay-deep-dive)). Google Research Football makes the same choice concretely. A full gauge means "60 m in the stick direction" (`manualTarget = playerPos + dir·clamp(power·60, 1, 100)`), the high pass uses `pow(gauge, 0.55)`, and the auto-target blend weights the stick target heavily (high-pass auto-direction 0.2) ([GRF AIfunctions.cpp:1049, 1088-1112](https://github.com/google-research/football/blob/3d9e754720a95621bba6475c4d3b0d56fe919014/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/AIsupport/AIfunctions.cpp); [gamedefines.hpp](https://github.com/google-research/football/blob/3d9e754720a95621bba6475c4d3b0d56fe919014/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/gamedefines.hpp)). Konami's line runs parallel. The PES 2019 manual says the "power gauge will decide how powerful a pass is" ([Konami PES 2019 manual](https://dds.konami.com/games/manual/pes2019/PC/en/control_advance.html), snippet). PES lets the stick reshape a lofted ball while the button is held ([realsport101](https://realsport101.com/article/pes-2021-controls-complete-guide-goalkeeper-defence-attack-on-ps4-and-xbox-one-dribbling-passing-shooting-tactics-gameplay-season-update), snippet). eFootball's "Stunning Lofted Pass" is officially "a lower and faster pass compared to a normal lofted pass", and stunning kicks "take longer to leave the player's feet" ([Konami eFootball controls](https://www.konami.com/efootball/en-us/page/new_controls)). That is the same "driven class costs something" trade FIFA makes. No official bar-to-distance formula was found for any EA or Konami title. The convention is clear even so: the meter means distance or power, the snap is assistance, and height is a separate class.

For receiving, **every system lets context pick the body part**. FC 26's notes list the inputs to first-touch selection as "ball speed relative to the player's speed, ball height, requested exit angle, opponent pressure". They also say that holding the control modifier "earlier" gives more control ([EA FC 26 deep dive](https://www.ea.com/games/ea-sports-fc/fc-26/news/pitch-notes-fc26-gameplay-deep-dive)). In FIFA 20 the pass, lob and shoot buttons double as header and volley, depending on ball height ([fifauteam manual](https://fifauteam.com/fifa-21-manual/)). PES 2018's Real Touch+ added trapping with the chest, stomach and thighs "depending on the scenario" ([Operation Sports](https://operationsports.com/pes-2018-features-strategic-dribbling-real-touch-new-set-pieces)). Madden's catch types are the cleanest precedent: the player picks Possession/Aggressive/RAC *intent* while the ball is in the air, and the game picks the animation ([EA Madden 26 guide](https://www.ea.com/games/madden-nfl/madden-nfl-26/tips-and-tricks-hub/m26-how-to-catch-the-ball)).

The open-source code shows the machinery. **swos-port** classifies ball height into five exclusive bands once per update and runs an ordered decision list. A kick needs the lowest band; fire at a ball in the upper bands starts a jump or static header; a static header can turn at most 90° toward the stick, read at contact ([swos-port](https://github.com/zlatkok/swos-port), `playerUpdateCore.cpp:104-138`, `updateAllPlayers.cpp:64-74, 195-217`, `player.cpp:383`). **YSoccer** adds the key refinement: it decides header vs. tackle from the ball's height **at the predicted frame the player can first reach it**, not its current height ([YSoccer](https://sourceforge.net/p/ysoccer/code/), `PlayerStateStandRun.java:79-104`, `Player.java:283-305`). **GRF** has no explicit body-part logic at all ([humancontroller.cpp](https://github.com/google-research/football/blob/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/player/controller/humancontroller.cpp), [humanoid.cpp](https://github.com/google-research/football/blob/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/player/humanoid/humanoid.cpp)). The controller pushes a priority queue every frame: buffered pass/shot, then ball control, trap, interfere, movement. The humanoid takes the first intent for which some clip's authored touch point matches the predicted ball at that clip's touch frame. Headers, chest traps and volleys "emerge" because those clips touch at head, chest or knee height. Five details of GRF carry over directly. The buffer lives until 2 s after release (indefinitely while held) and is cancelled when time-to-ball worsens by more than 700 ms. A pending pass shrinks trap acceptance by ×0.3, so first-time play wins whenever it fits. While a pass is buffered, the control touch is biased toward braking "to set up" that pass. Clip choice freezes in the last 6 frames (60 ms) before contact. And a sloppy contact blends the intended outgoing velocity with the incoming one ("bumpyRideBias").

The criticism of commercial games is just as instructive. Players complain that aerial balls "go flying out of the stadium" instead of being brought down, that first touches pop the ball up, and that the game produces flicks or lobs they never pressed ([EA Forums FC 24](https://forums.ea.com/discussions/fc-24-technical-issues-en/players-can%e2%80%99t-take-a-touch-on-eafc24/8023339); [EA Forums FC 27](https://forums.ea.com/idea/fc-27-bug-reports-en/ground-passing-non-functional/13716188/comments/13716712); snippet-level evidence). Fighting games document the matching failure of buffers: buffered actions fire after the situation has changed ([SmashWiki](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Buffer)). The lesson for WildPitch is that **error must be explainable from visible causes** (pace, height, pressure, awkward exit angle), height thresholds need hysteresis, and a buffer needs situational cancels, not just a timer. On rendering, every AAA source describes the same approach: the sim decides the contact, and the animation is warped so the limb arrives there on the right frame. EA's GDC talk applies warping to "any animation that must reach a specific point at a specific time" ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1012342/Animation-Warping-for-Responsiveness-in)). Unreal's skew warp ends its window on the strike frame ([Unreal docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/motion-warping-in-unreal-engine)). GRF eases a positional "smuggle" into the body before its touch frame.

## Physics gives each stance a speed budget

One rigid-body impulse law covers every contact. For incoming normal speed u, body-segment speed v_b toward the ball, effective impact mass M, ball mass m and restitution e, **v_out = ((e·M − m)·u + (1+e)·M·v_b)/(M + m)**. With the measured head-torso impact mass of 9.94 kg and e = 0.70 ± 0.11, it predicts 6.0 m/s for a header measured at 6.42 m/s ([Kristensen & Terp 2001](https://ojs.ub.uni-konstanz.de/cpa/article/view/3900/3617)). For heading this reduces to **v_out ≈ 0.63·u + 1.63·v_head**. Lab headers of an 8 m/s ball add 1.5–3.0 m/s, and trunk angular velocity is the only significant predictor ([Ludwig et al. 2001](https://ojs.ub.uni-konstanz.de/cpa/article/view/3901/3618)). For the foot, Dörge's 18.6 m/s foot speed giving 24.7 m/s ball speed (COR 0.58) implies an effective foot mass near 2.4 kg [derived], so **v_out ≈ 0.36·u + 1.36·v_foot**. A volley therefore gains only 3–4 m/s even from a 10 m/s ball meeting the foot head-on ([Dörge et al. 2002](https://people.stfx.ca/smackenz/Courses/HK474/Midterm/Dorge%202002%20Biomechanical%20differences%20in%20soccer%20kicking%20with%20the%20preferred%20and%20non-preferred%20leg.pdf)). The firm, passive bounds are known. A ball off a rigid plate has e ≈ 0.82–0.88 (FIFA rebound spec, via a [retailer listing](https://decathlon.ie/p/12895-327588-thermobonded-size-5-football-f900-fifa-pro-white-green-blue.html)), off the head 0.7, and off a passive foot about 0.5–0.6. Cushioning works by withdrawing the segment (v_b < 0) and lengthening contact; an absorbent layer alone cut COR by 21.7% in one test ([Taha et al. 2013](https://libmast.utm.my/Record/UMPir5533/Description)). Coaching describes the same move: drop the thigh, and pull the chest back or arch it at contact so the ball falls to the feet ([soccerxpert](https://www.soccerxpert.com/tips/details/chest-traps); [MOJO chest](https://mojo.sport/coachs-corner/how-to-receive-a-soccer-ball-with-your-chest)). For glancing contacts, Rod Cross's bounce model separates normal from tangential restitution; tangential speed is mostly kept at shallow incidence ([Cross](https://www.physics.usyd.edu.au/~cross/BOUNCE.htm)). That is why a flick-on keeps most of the ball's pace.

The surface used depends on height at arrival. Coaching sources agree that the instep, thigh, chest and head are used, and that "the height of the ball as it reaches the player usually decides which one" ([Soccer Coach Weekly](https://soccercoachweekly.net/coaching-advice/what-is-receiving); [FA Boot Room](https://www.thefa.com/bootroom/resources/coaching/how-to-help-players-improve-their-receiving-skills)). The thigh takes a *dropping* ball below the chest; the chest takes a dropping or driven ball above the abdomen. No source gives numeric thresholds, so the band edges below are proportional estimates for a 1.80 m player [D].

Lofted-ball ballistics are well measured. A football passes through its drag crisis at about 10–14 m/s. It is launched at 15–30 m/s with C_D ≈ 0.15–0.25, then slows into the high-drag regime near landing, so it comes down steeper than it went up. Spin decays only 4–8% by the apex, and backspin lift plateaus at C_L ≈ 0.3 ([Goff & Carré 2009](https://wiki.penfieldrobotics.com/wiki/images/c/c0/Goff_Carre_AJP_2009.pdf)). WildPitch's single drag constant, 0.013, equals the supercritical value (β·C_D ≈ 0.053 × 0.22–0.25), so long lobs come out slightly long and flat compared with reality. That is acceptable here and keeps the solver simple. Long throw-ins of 12–19 m/s at 22–40° bracket realistic lofted launches ([Linthorne & Thomas](https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/handle/2438/12719); [review](https://digitum.um.es/digitum/bitstream/10201/151565/1/586031.pdf)).

## Recommendations: one planner, a band table, a buffer and a meter

### The lob meter: keep D-045's controls, fix what it inherited

D-045's controls should stay. The tap keeps the quick auto lob, a hold beyond 12 ticks drives the meter, the slide keeps priority on the press edge, and the held-ball branch keeps priority after it. A linear mapping from hold to distance is the readable choice: GRF's power exponent exists because its gauge means *power*, not metres. The changes are all in what the meter feeds:

**Scale the range to the pitch.** Use D(h) = D_min + (D_max − D_min)·h with D_min = 12 m (just past the tap, as now) and D_max = min(45 m, 0.62 × pitch length) [D]. That gives 45 m on the 80 m 7v7 pitch, 37 m on the 60 m 5v5 pitch and 25 m on the 40 m regression pitch. Moving the 7v7 top end to 50 m is a feel call.

**Snap on predicted points.** Each team-mate in the stick cone gets a lead point P_j = q_j + q̇_j·t_c(d_j), using the flight-time table below. That is GRF's distance rating applied to predicted rather than current positions. Choose the mate minimising |P_j − land| + 6 m·(angle/π). Accept him if the miss is within today's 4 m + 12%, which is about GRF's tolerance [C/D]. Inside that tolerance the ball lands on P_j, with the meter's surplus or shortfall applied as a depth bias clamped to ±3 m along his run, so a slightly long ball plays him into space. Outside it, the lob stays untargeted.

**Keep every landing on the pitch.** Clamp targeted and untargeted landings alike by the through-ball margins, 3 m inside the byline and 1.5 m inside the touchline (`T_THROUGH_MARGIN_X/Y`), as D-044 intended. Hanging the snap tolerance on the per-seat `assist_level` is optional: Off = pure distance, Normal = today's tolerance, High = ×1.5.

The trajectory change is tiny. **Remove the vz clamp and use vz = 5.0 + 0.245·D** [P]. The existing (d, vz) → vh bisection then produces a launch angle that eases from 45° at 10 m to 35° at 50 m, with no runtime trigonometry:

| D | vz | vh | Angle | Flight to grass | Apex | Ball at z = 1.0 m (descending): time, speed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 m | 7.45 | 7.4 | 45° | 1.47 s | 2.6 m | 1.31 s, 8.5 m/s |
| 15 | 8.68 | 9.9 | 41° | 1.69 | 3.5 | 1.56 s, 10.6 |
| 20 | 9.90 | 12.2 | 39° | 1.90 | 4.4 | 1.78 s, 12.2 |
| 25 | 11.1 | 14.3 | 38° | 2.11 | 5.4 | 2.00 s, 13.5 |
| 30 | 12.4 | 16.3 | 37° | 2.30 | 6.5 | 2.20 s, 14.5 |
| 40 | 14.8 | 20.3 | 36° | 2.66 | 8.7 | 2.58 s, 16.2 |
| 50 | 17.3 | 24.5 | 35° | 2.99 | 11.0 | 2.91 s, 17.4 |

These sit inside the real envelope. A drag-and-spin model anchored on Goff & Carré puts 25 m lofted passes at 1.8–2.6 s and 40 m passes at 2.3–3.5 s [physics note, own model]. Note that the bisection's `hi` bounds vh, not launch speed. The launch speed √(vh² + vz²) reaches 27.6 m/s at 45 m and 30 m/s at 50 m, within measured kick speeds, so D_max is the effective cap. The 12 m short end now clears a defender's head (apex about 3 m). If 2.8–3 s of hang time proves too slow for long switches, flatten the long end: vz = 5 + 0.2·D gives roughly 30° there. That is a feel call.

**The lead must use the time to receiving height, not the grass landing.** D-045's 0.92·2·vz/g estimates landing, and it drifts once vz is unclamped. Generalise `ball_carry_distance` into `ball_flight_to(vh, vz, z0, z_end, &dist, &ticks)`. It is the same loop, starting at z0 and stopping when the ball *descends* through z_end. The throw-in's arrive-at-0.9 m solve is the in-code precedent [C `src/sim/player.c:340-350`]. The targeted lob then solves for the ball crossing z_end = 1.0 m at P = q + q̇·t_c, iterating t_c three times as now. The pass handoff passes P and t_c to `seats_pass_handoff` instead of the default meet [C `src/sim/player.c:396`]. The lofted ball therefore drops onto a running receiver at thigh-to-chest height, and the contact planner below decides what he does with it.

**Make it a table, not a search.** Once vz is a function of D alone, vh(D), t_c(D) and the descent speed depend only on D. Fill a 64-entry table (0.75 m steps from 0 to 48 m) once at init, using the same fixed-point `ball_flight_to`, and interpolate linearly. The table is computed deterministically and immutable afterwards. The snap then costs a lookup per mate per iteration instead of a 16-step bisection, and a presentation-side landing ghost ring, if the owner wants one, can read the same table through a `const SimState*` every frame without touching the sim.

A driven-lob class, about 22°, flat and fast, is the obvious second class: FIFA, PES and eFootball all have one. Which input triggers it, a modifier or Sensible-Soccer-style stick-against-run ([Giant Bomb](https://www.giantbomb.com/wd/3030-27453), snippet), is the owner's call. The AI should use the same path, calling the D-based solve directly with D = |target − ball| clamped to [D_min, D_max], so nothing negative or out of range reaches the table. AI crosses use the driven class (below). The avatar should pose the lob strike with `kick_power01 = h` instead of today's fixed 0.5.

### Action selection: bands at the contact tick, a contact plan, a fallback order

The organising rule, taken from YSoccer and GRF, is that **the body part is a consequence of the ball's height at the planned contact tick, never of its current height**. Build it as a generalisation of `header.c`, whose functions already form a one-row version of the pipeline. `ball_approach` becomes a shared reach scan, `header_wanted` becomes the HEAD row of a table, `header_start/step` becomes a generic committed contact action, `header_contact_dist` becomes a band-aware window, and `header_contact` becomes per-kind ball response [C `src/sim/header.c`]. That makes a new module, `src/sim/contact.c` with a `contact_internal.h`, which fits the one-responsibility rule.

**Prediction.** Compute a ball prediction ring of up to 180 ticks (3 s, GRF's `ballPredictionSize_ms`) by calling the *same* air, bounce and board step the integrator uses, without player collisions. `header.c`'s analytic, drag-free parabola is acceptable for a 0.45 m head window. It is not acceptable for chest, thigh and volley windows on 3 s lobs. Keep the ring out of `SimState`, because it is derived data. Recompute it on the stack of `sim_step` each tick, and only when it is needed: the ball is above 0.25 m or bouncing, or some plan is active. Stop the scan at the first tick where the ball rolls below 0.25 m with no further bounce. Measure the cost against the 17.7 µs tick in `bench_sim`. If it costs more than 10%, add a cache that is valid across sim instances (replays, tests): a static ring keyed on the *complete* input to the prediction (ball position, velocity, z, vz, spin and the pitch dimensions), reused only on exact fixed-point equality of that key. A stack buffer cannot persist between ticks, and a static one shared by several sims is safe only with a complete key.

**Reach scan and bands.** For each player that matters this tick (the pass target, seat-controlled players and AI players in chase), find the earliest ring tick t at which `dist_xy(ball(t), pos) ≤ reach + v_reach·(t − t_react)`. Use `meet_choose`'s reach model: 0.8125 × sprint speed after 0.15 s. Then read ball_z(t) and evaluate the next few ticks too, because waiting for a dropping ball is often the better choice. The bands are proposed tunables, scaled by player height later [D]:

| Band | Ball-centre z at contact | Default (CONTROL) stance | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| GROUND | ≤ 0.25 m | sole / inside-foot trap (today's `gain_apply`) | unchanged path, D-039 tuning kept |
| LOW | 0.25–0.55 | instep / inside cushion | = today's feet band; volleys and half-volleys live here |
| MID | 0.55–1.00 | thigh (descending ball) | knee ≈ 0.51 m on a 1.80 m player (anthropometric fraction, unverified) |
| CHEST | 1.00–1.50 | chest (lean-back if descending, sink if driven) | top moves from 1.45 to 1.50 |
| HEAD | 1.45–2.05 | standing header | overlap 1.45–1.50 resolved by intent: CONTROL → chest, PASS/SHOT/LOB → head |
| JUMP | 2.05–2.60 | jumping header (`WP_JUMP_VZ` 3.4, ~0.59 m apex) | as today |

**Candidate table** (band × buffered intent). This replaces nested ifs, and every row is a few tunables:

| Band | No intent / CONTROL | PASS (tap / through) | SHOT | LOB |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GROUND | trap | first-time pass | first-time shot | first-time lob |
| LOW | instep cushion | half-volley pass | volley | volleyed lob / clearance |
| MID | thigh | volley pass if easy, else thigh + keep buffer | volley (side-on) | volleyed clearance, else thigh + keep |
| CHEST | chest | chest lay-off if a mate ≤ 8 m in a ±60° cone, else chest + keep | chest + keep (→ volley on the drop) | chest + keep |
| HEAD | header-control if ball ≤ 11 m/s, else none (body cylinder) | header pass | header shot | lofted header / flick-on |
| JUMP | none | jumping header pass | jumping header shot | jumping lofted header / clearance |

**Utility and fallback order.** Each candidate (kind, t) is scored D-008 style in fixed point: score = 8·intent_met − 4·difficulty − 1·(t − t_earliest)/30 − 2·pressure(t), with weights to tune [D]. Difficulty is defined in the physics section. Like GRF's ×0.3 on traps, a first-time candidate that satisfies the buffered intent wins whenever its difficulty is acceptable. The resulting **fallback order** is: (1) first-time execution of the buffered intent at the earliest reachable tick; (2) the same intent at a later tick within 20 ticks, letting the ball drop to a better band, if no opponent reaches it first; (3) a control touch at the earliest tick, *keeping the intent buffered*, so the chain fires on the next touch; (4) movement only, steering to the plan's contact point. Ties break in fixed candidate order, never by RNG.

**The contact plan** is committed into `WpPlayer`: `{u8 kind, u8 band, u8 flags (jump, stretch, committed), u16 contact_tick, fx contact_x, contact_y, contact_z, fx quality}`, about 20 bytes per player. Most of the action bookkeeping reuses `p->action`, `action_timer` and `p->kick`. Re-planning is allowed every tick until `contact_tick − now ≤ T_CONTACT_COMMIT` = **5 ticks** (83 ms; GRF freezes 60 ms) [S for the idea, D for the value]. After that the plan is frozen, so the presentation has a stable target and nobody can exploit last-instant swaps. At the contact tick, test the ball's swept segment over the tick against a sphere around the stance's body point. `gain_check` already sweeps reach over the tick for ground balls [C `src/sim/player.c:600-636`]. A miss means the ball continues and the plan clears.

**The intent buffer per seat** sits in `SimState` beside `seat_player[]`: `{u8 intent, u8 held, u16 gauge_ticks, u16 age_ticks, i16 ttb_at_press}` per seat. A press edge of PASS/SHOOT/LOB records the intent; the newest press overwrites, which avoids the stale-buffer failure SmashWiki documents. The gauge accumulates while the button is held. Age counts from release, as in GRF.

The buffer is cancelled when age exceeds **90 ticks (1.5 s)** [D; GRF uses 2 s]. It is also cancelled when the predicted ticks-to-ball grows by more than **42 ticks** (GRF's 700 ms), when an opponent gains possession, when the seat switches player, or when no reachable contact exists within 3 s. A buffered LOB whose ball an opponent wins is cancelled, never converted into a slide: a slide needs a fresh press, so the game never does something nobody pressed.

When the contact tick arrives while the button is still held, use the gauge value at that tick, as GRF does once its 500 ms threshold has passed [S]. Otherwise, a held lob would always degrade into a control touch. The intent is resolved (cleared) only when an executed kind satisfies it. A control touch leaves it pending. AI players need no buffer: `brain.c` writes the same intent value directly into the plan's input, so humans and AI share one planner.

Both the buffer and the plan live in `SimState`. **They must be hashed in `sim_hash()`, and `WP_SIMSTATE_SIZE_EXPECTED` must be updated** (CLAUDE.md hard rule). Every new constant goes in `tuning.h`, including `header.c`'s `HEAD_Z`, `HEAD_REACH_*` and `WAIT_MAX`, the header-pass 0.7·d + 3 and vz 1.8, and `gain_apply`'s 0.45 spill and 6-tick cooldown. All of them currently violate the rule [C].

### Per-stance contact physics in Q16.16

**Control touches use an outcome model with a physics residual.** Fixed surface normals in a pure impulse model proved fragile. With plausible normals, my prototype dropped a chest-trapped 20 m lob 1.4–1.7 m away and a knock-down header 3.7–3.9 m away [P]. Both miss the target envelope, and the head-control pop (apex 2.1–3.4 m) would break ownership. So model a cushion as what the player intends plus what he fails to absorb. The clean outgoing velocity is solved ballistically: v_z,ideal = √(2·g·h_pop), and v_h,ideal = d_ready / t_ready along the exit direction, plus the player's own velocity. Here t_ready is the fall time from z_contact with that v_z to z = 0.30 m. That needs one `fx_sqrt` per touch. The exit direction is the stick at the touch, or the stick held within the last 12 ticks. That is a new buffer: D-039 already stops a held stick near reception from cancelling the assist [C `src/sim/seats.c:158-167`], but `gain_apply` ignores the stick. By default the exit is toward the facing. If a pass or shot is buffered, it biases toward the facing so the next touch can strike, as GRF does. The residual is v_res = (1 − q)·e_firm·mirror(r), where r is the 3-D relative velocity and mirror() reflects it about the stance's nominal normal (an FX constant pair per stance). The result is v_out = v_ideal + v_res, followed by an angular error of σ·(1 − q) drawn once, in its own statement. The model recovers the physics. A perfect cushion of a 15 m/s ball returns about 1.7 m/s, an effective e ≈ 0.11, inside the 0.1–0.3 cushioned estimate. A failed one tends toward the firm passive e ≈ 0.5–0.7 that the data bound.

Touch quality is q = clamp((1 − over − 0.6·setup_deficit − 0.3·pressure − stretch)·(1 + 0.25·k), 0, 1), where k = `stat_k(CONTROL)` ∈ [−0.98, 0.98] (50 = 0, the D-034 convention) [C `src/sim/stats.c`]. Here over = max(0, |r| − v_clean)/v_clean uses the **3-D** relative speed. setup_deficit = max(0, setup_ticks − ticks_planned)/setup_ticks. pressure is 1 when an opponent is within 1.5 m, falling to 0 at 4 m. stretch is the existing 0.3·(d − 1.0)/0.3 stretch spill. Above 1.6·v_clean there is no control: the body cylinder deflects the ball as today. **Speed is graded, not a gate.** EA documents the same principle: its driven lobs carry higher receiver trap error based on distance and speed ([EA Advanced Passing](https://www.ea.com/able/news/gameplay-advanced-passing)). Running the lob table's arrival speeds (8.5–17.4 m/s) through this formula with no pressure and a full setup gives the following [P]. At k = 0, chest touches stay clean at every lob distance (drop 0.6–0.7 m). Thigh touches stay clean to 14.5 m/s and drop at most 1.3 m at 17.4 m/s. Instep touches grow heavy from about 13.5 m/s, reaching a 1.2 m drop at 17.4 m/s. All of these stay well inside the 2.2 m `T_LOSE_DIST`. Header-control drops 2.4 m at 13.5 m/s and 6 m at 17.4 m/s. That is a loss, so the planner's difficulty term steers fast high balls to the chest or to a header. A k = 0.28 player (stat 64) shrinks these by about a third.

| Stance | h_pop | d_ready | Clean out speed / t to z 0.55 [P] | Apex (contact z) | e_firm | v_clean | setup ticks | Nominal normal (elev.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sole / ground | 0 | 0.30 m | today's gain_apply | — | 0.5 | 16 m/s | 12 | — |
| Instep cushion (LOW) | 0.05 m | 0.35 | 1.6 m/s / already in band | 0.45 (0.40) | 0.5 | 13 | 12 | 70° |
| Thigh (MID) | 0.12 | 0.50 | 1.8 / 0.41 s | 0.87 (0.75) | 0.55 | 15 | 18 | 60° |
| Chest (CHEST) | 0.10 | 0.60 | 1.7 / 0.55–0.61 s | 1.35–1.60 (1.25–1.50) | 0.55 | 17 | 24 | 35° descending, 5° driven |

All the stance values are [D] estimates, chosen so that the clean result lands in the physics note's chest envelope, "lands about 0.4–0.6 s later about 0.5–1.2 m in front". The ball pops above the 1.45 m ownership ceiling for chest contacts above 1.35 m and for header control. So an aerial control touch sets the owner plus a hashed **`settle_ticks`** counter (one byte per player). For that many ticks, the full t_ready, `control_owner` keeps possession up to z ≤ 2.1 m and 2.2 m distance. Dribble touches resume when the ball enters the feet band, exactly as today's early return does [C `src/sim/player.c:535-548`]. Opponents can still challenge the settling ball through the normal tackle path, as FIFA 22 allows during "an opponent's flick up or chest trap" ([EA FIFA 22 pitch notes](https://www.ea.com/ea-play/news/pitch-notes-fifa-22-gameplay-deep-dive)).

**Headers** use the measured law. For a target direction ŵ (3-D, from the pass solve below), the contact normal is the bisector n = norm(ŵ − r̂). Then u_n = −r·n, and the normal and tangential parts combine in quadrature: s_max = √((0.63·u_n + 1.63·v_head)² + (0.85·|r_t|)²) [S for the 0.63/1.63 coefficients; D for the tangential 0.85 retention]. v_head is at most 2.5 m/s standing and 3.5 jumping for passes and shots, and 5 for clearances. If the required speed exceeds s_max, the ball falls short, a readable consequence. Two families separate cleanly by φ, the angle between ŵ and the direction back toward where the ball came from. *Facing headers* (φ ≤ 90°, SWOS's limit) get the full budget, with error σ_h·(1 + φ/90°)·(1 + over). *Flick-ons* (φ ≥ 135°) keep 80–95% of the speed and may deflect the path by at most 40°. The 90–135° range is awkward, with error tripled [D]. These give header passes of 8–13 m/s from 10–15 m/s balls and headed shots of 13–18 m/s from crosses, consistent with the physics note's derivation. **The header pass gets a real solve**: the lead point P comes from the mate's motion and `ball_flight_to` from z0 = 1.8 m to z_end = 0.3 m. The vz class depends on distance: a nod-down to feet (vz = −1…0) up to 6 m, then vz = 1.0 + 0.15·d above that. This replaces "at his current position, vz 1.8" [C `src/sim/header.c:119-120`]. A LOB intent at head height gives the lofted header: vz from the lob formula, with the header budget as the cap.

**Volleys** use the foot law. Shot speed = min(T_SHOT_MAX, 0.9·v_shot(h) + 0.36·max(0, −r·ŵ)), so volleys run 0–15% under a set shot, as the physics note infers. Aim error is 2× the ground shot at LOW and 3× at MID, scaled by (1 + over). A vertical bias of +0.35 rad per metre of contact height above 0.45 m makes a 0.9 m volley rise about 9° more and sail over unless it is struck down [D]. **First-time passes from the air** stay simple. A LOW-band half-volley pass is struck with vz = −0.5 m/s at the ground-pass speed × 1.05, so it meets the grass within about 0.2 s and the bounce model returns it to a roll. A MID-band volley pass is allowed only when |r| ≤ 10 m/s and the exit is within 60° of facing. A chest lay-off uses the chest's firm budget, 0.55·|r_n| + 1.5 m/s, capped at 6 m/s toward a mate within 8 m.

**Chains then fall out of the buffer.** *Chest then pass*: the PASS intent stays buffered through the chest touch, so the planner's next candidate is a GROUND or LOW contact 0.55–0.6 s later. Because `pass_solve` assumes a rolling ball, the default waits for the feet band and strikes a ground or half-volley pass rather than a pass from 1 m up. That fixes today's wrong pace. *Chest then volley*: a buffered SHOT takes the first reachable tick at or below 1.0 m on the drop, which is the classic chest-and-volley. *Header pass*: PASS buffered at HEAD band. *First-time lob or clearance*: LOB buffered at GROUND or LOW. *Flick-up then volley* needs a control modifier that does not exist yet, so it is deferred.

### AI and rendering stay on the same contract

The AI uses the same entry points. `brain.c`'s lob, cross and restart calls use the D-based lob solve, with D clamped to the meter range, so AI and human lobs share one solver. Today they take the tap path with no meter [C `src/sim/brain.c:549, 662`]. Crosses should use the driven class with z_end = 1.8 m at the target point. In the driven class a 20 m ball flies 1.34 s with a 2.2 m apex [P], so it arrives at heading height instead of landing on the striker. Receiving AI players set their intent on the decision beat, using D-027's existing rules: header at goal within 14 m, clearance in their own third, otherwise nod on or control. The planner then picks the stance, which removes the AI's current "head whenever `header_wanted`" bias. A keeper "hands" row (catch up to 2.4 m with a jump) is the natural next row of the table. Today `T_KEEPER_HANDS_Z` = 1.45 m means a keeper cannot claim a cross except by a shot-triggered dive [C]. That row belongs in its own checkpoint.

Rendering only reads. The avatar maps `plan.kind` to a procedural pose family. Chest: lean back, arms out. Thigh: knee raise and withdraw. Instep: foot raised to the ball then withdrawn. Volley: side-on hip swing. Headers keep their existing pose. The pose phase is 1 − (contact_tick − now)/setup_ticks, so the limb peaks exactly on the contact tick, the procedural equivalent of a skew warp ending on the strike frame. Frozen plans give a stable target, and earlier re-plans cross-fade in cosmetic, non-sim state. For a post-contact recovery pose, read `settle_ticks` and the last contact kind, a hashed byte. Extend `--check-kick-pose` for every stance: the posed limb must be within 0.15 m of `contact_point` at `contact_tick`.

## Conclusion

The research changes what "aerial control" means for this codebase. It is not a set of new animations. It is one missing quantity, **the ball's state at the tick the player can actually meet it**. With that quantity, the lob's lead, the receiver's target point, the body part, the error and the pose all derive from the same prediction. Without it, each feature needs its own constant and its own special case, and the code already shows the symptoms: a meter whose snap looks at where team-mates *are*, vz clamped so that long lobs become 36 m/s drives, header passes aimed where the mate *was*, and a 2-D speed check blind to a lob's descent. The same structure is also the long-term hook for learned brains. D-008's "candidate actions × utility" now covers every touch, so a trained policy can later replace the scoring weights without touching the physics.

The evidence also exposed an honest limit. The literature constrains the *firm* end of every contact (head e ≈ 0.7, foot ≈ 0.5–0.6) and the header and kick impulse laws. It says nothing quantitative about how well a good player cushions a ball. That end of every stance is a design choice dressed in physics. That is why the recommended model expresses it as an intended outcome plus a physically bounded residual. The playtest dials, d_ready, h_pop, v_clean and the quality weights, stay legible, while the extremes stay real.
