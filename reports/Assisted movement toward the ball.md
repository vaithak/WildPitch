# Bend the stick toward the meeting point

WildPitch should add a **stick-gated "chase cone"**: when the ball is loose and the stick points roughly at it, the human player's heading is turned, by at most 30°, onto the predicted point where he can meet the ball, while speed, sprint, aim and actions stay his and a neutral stick still means stand still. The games we studied all decide where the controlled man should go by *time to the predicted ball*, not distance to where it is now; Google Research Football steers the human's legs with an on-by-default "magnet", while SWOS and YSoccer leave the legs raw and put all their help into switching and AI runners, and EA FC and eFootball publish settings but no numbers. WildPitch today helps only the receiver of a pass its own human kicked, plus a 30-tick chase after an auto-switch, so every loose ball, clearance, deflection, untargeted long ball and every pass *from an AI team-mate to the human* runs on the raw stick. With a 1.0 m control reach a ball 2.5 m away is missed at about 24° off line, and an 8-way keyboard can be 22.5° off any bearing, which is why the owner feels the player "not going to the ball". The recommended default is full strength within **30°** fading to exactly zero at **55°**, applied only to the man whose ball it is, together with fixes for AI-made passes and time-based switching, and backed by tests proving that a neutral or clearly-away stick behaves exactly as with the assist off. No shipped game publishes its angles, so every number here is a WildPitch starting value derived from its own geometry, to be settled by playtest.

## Every engine steers to the meeting point, never to the ball

The open-source engines are the only places where the mechanism can be read rather than inferred, and they span the full design space. **SWOS never moves the human's selected man on its own.** A neutral joystick calls `stopPlayer`, and a held direction moves him one 8-way step ([SWOS updateAllPlayers.cpp](https://github.com/zlatkok/swos-port/blob/master/src/game/updatePlayers/updateAllPlayers.cpp)). The help comes from elsewhere. Control auto-switches to whoever is nearest the ball by squared pixel distance. A second, CPU-driven team-mate is sent after the ball's *current* position, which is pure pursuit with no prediction for outfielders. He is handed control once he is within the pickup radius, followed by a 25-tick switch cooldown ([SWOS playerUpdateCore.cpp](https://github.com/zlatkok/swos-port/blob/master/src/game/updatePlayers/playerUpdateCore.cpp); [SWOS swos.asm](https://github.com/zlatkok/swos-port/blob/master/swos/swos.asm)). **YSoccer is equally raw on the legs.** Its speed is zero whenever the input device has no value, and otherwise the player runs exactly along the stick angle ([YS PlayerStateStandRun.java](https://sourceforge.net/p/ysoccer/code/ci/master/tree/java/core/src/main/java/com/ygames/ysoccer/match/PlayerStateStandRun.java)). Its switching is smarter than SWOS's, though. Every player gets a "frames to reach the ball" from a 2-second ball prediction. Control moves only when the nearest man needs less than **0.5×** the controlled man's frames. The pass recipient is protected ("passing mate, let him get the ball"), so he runs to the predicted intercept under AI control until he touches it ([YS Team.java](https://sourceforge.net/p/ysoccer/code/ci/master/tree/java/core/src/main/java/com/ygames/ysoccer/match/Team.java); [YS AiStateSeeking.java](https://sourceforge.net/p/ysoccer/code/ci/master/tree/java/core/src/main/java/com/ygames/ysoccer/match/AiStateSeeking.java)).

**Google Research Football is the one engine that steers the human's own man, and its design is the template here.** `use_magnet = true` is the default, with the comment "automatically pushes active player towards the ball" ([GRF main.hpp](https://github.com/google-research/football/blob/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/main.hpp)). `PlayerController::_MovementCommand` computes `manual·(1−autoBias) + auto·autoBias`. The auto vector comes from `AI_GetToBallMovement`, which searches a 3-second ball prediction in 10 ms steps for reachable intercepts below 1 m height. It rates them 0.4 on velocity match and 0.6 on direction match with the stick, and runs at `timeNeeded/time × maxVelocity` so the player arrives just in time ([GRF playercontroller.cpp](https://github.com/google-research/football/blob/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/player/controller/playercontroller.cpp); [GRF AIfunctions.cpp](https://github.com/google-research/football/blob/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/AIsupport/AIfunctions.cpp)). The weight is 1 for the team's designated ball-winner. For anyone else it grows with agreement between stick and auto direction: `sameDirFactor = 1 − |angle|/π`, remapped to 0.5–1. It is multiplied by `(1 − inputDirIsOwnHalfFactor)` under the comment "if we run for our own half, don't accidentally magnet somewhere", and boosted for 300 ms after a switch because "when we get passed a ball while we are not the designated player … we want to at least try to get there". Apart from the own-half cancel and possession conditions, we could verify no hard angular cut-off in GRF's partial magnet (its `curve()` shaping was not read). That is the one thing WildPitch should deliberately do differently, because only a weight that is exactly zero beyond a fixed angle can pass a "bit-identical when pointing away" test.

The commercial games confirm the shape but hide the numbers. EA has no "run to the ball" setting. The relevant pieces are scattered. **Auto Switching** offers Air Balls and Loose Balls, Auto, or Manual. **Auto Switching Move Assistance** "keeps the player who you have auto switched to moving in their current direction for a period of time", at levels None/Low/Medium/High; FIFA 21 defaults it to None ([EA Accessibility FIFA 21](https://www.ea.com/able/resources/fifa/fifa-21/xbox-one/controller-settings); [ginx](https://www.ginx.tv/en/fifa-21-controller-settings-tutorial), snippet). **Pass Block Assistance** makes user-controlled players "attempt to intercept a pass automatically when the ball is close by" ([fifauteam FC 26](https://fifauteam.com/fc-26-controller-settings/)). EA's own pitch notes say an FC 24 Precision Pass receiver "will attempt to move toward the ball's destination" ([EA FC 24 deep dive](https://www.ea.com/games/ea-sports-fc/pitch-notes/news/fc-24-gameplay-deepdive), snippet). EA also once fixed switched defenders "unintentionally running towards the ball instead of following the receiver" on lofted balls ([EA FIFA Mobile, Aug 2022](https://ea.com/games/fifa/fifa-mobile/amp/news/gameplay-improvements-august-2022), snippet). That is an official admission that an unconditional pull toward the ball is wrong for defenders. Konami's design is "default run plus override verbs". In Winning Eleven 9, R1+R2 meant "aborting the run to the ball" ([gamepressure WE9](https://www.gamepressure.com/worldsoccerwinningeleven9/control/zb281)). Modern eFootball's Cursor Change "Assisted" switches "in a more frequent manner" to a player "near the ball and more suited to the flow of the play" ([Konami v2.2.0 notes](https://www.konami.com/efootball/en/page/2023/season2_patch-notes_v2-20)). Kick Off and Sensible Soccer deliberately kept the ball off the feet ("the ball doesn't 'stick' to your players' feet"; [dazeland](https://dazeland.com/en/Amiga/Kick_Off2.html), snippet). Their accessibility came from nearest-man switching and pickup radii, as the SWOS source confirms. Community complaints repeat the same failures in mirror image: a switch to the man "underneath the ball who is not close to landing point" ([EA forum FC 24](https://forums.ea.com/discussions/fc-24-technical-issues-en/player-switching-auto-select-issues-after-latest-update/7985095), forum snippet), and receivers who let the ball "roll right by" ([EA forum FC Mobile](https://forums.ea.com/discussions/fc-mobile-general-discussion-en/passing-bug/8045033), forum snippet).

The aim-assist literature explains why the help should be bounded and mostly invisible. Madden 25's Heat-Seeker "guides your defender on the proper angle", fixes "small errors like oversteers", and aims to "provide assistance without taking control away from the user" (EA's Madden 25 defence post, quoted via a search snippet because the page now returns 404: [EA](https://www.ea.com/news/madden-25-defense); see also [ESPN Playbook](https://www.espn.com/blog/playbook/tech/post/_/id/4807/madden-25-makes-defensive-adjustments)). Witchfire defines an assist by an outer zone where it begins and an inner zone where it is full strength ([The Astronauts](https://www.theastronauts.com/2019/02/witchfire-aim-assist)). In the CHI 2014 FPS study, outcome-side assists such as bullet magnetism held up in real play, while some control-side ones were "too noticeable to players" ([Bateman et al.](https://scottbateman.github.io/publication/2014-01-01-The-effectiveness-or-lack-thereof-of-aim-assist-techniques-in-first-person-shooter-games)). One reason the game should do the prediction *for* the player is that humans intercepting decelerating targets make most of their early errors ([Port et al. 1997](https://darkwing.uoregon.edu/~prd/pdfs/Port_EBR97.pdf)), and a rolling ball is exactly such a target.

## WildPitch assists only the passes its own human kicked

The audit, read at commit 9898d7b plus the D-036 clock edits, finds movement assist in exactly three places. Function names are given because a parallel session is shifting line numbers. **First, the targeted receiver of a pass the human's own seat player kicked.** `seats_pass_handoff` records the pass, and `recv_update` plus `seats_assist_receive` steer the legs to `recv_goal`. The steering lasts until a takeover (stick more than 120° away for 15 ticks), SWITCH, the ball being out of reach for 6 ticks, or the pass ending. **Second, up to 30 ticks after an auto-switch**, but only if a stick was held at the switch and has not changed (`latch_ticks`, set in `do_switch`). **Third, a header the human has asked for** with PASS or SHOOT (`header_assist_move`). Outside these cases `move()` turns the raw stick into `dir × top × |stick|`, approached at 18 m/s². It has no steering toward an unowned ball. The only "step up to the ball" logic is gated on `ball.owner == i` (src/sim/player.c `move`). The file-header comment claims more than the code does.

The biggest gap is a one-line early return. **In `seats_pass_handoff`, a kicker who is not a seat's player sets `pass_stick = -128` and returns**, so a pass from an AI team-mate to the human's man gets no receive assist at all. The human keeps only the 1.3 m stretch reach. AI kicks do name a receiver: `do_kick` sets `k.to` for passes, throughs and lobs. So the information is there, and it is simply thrown away. The other gaps compound it. A short pass that dies below 1.0 m/s on the ground ends the pass, and with it the assist (`seats_update`). The takeover is one-way for the rest of the pass, so a 250 ms run loses the assist even if the player turns back. The latch cancels on *exact* stick inequality, so one unit of analogue jitter ends the chase. Loose-ball auto-switch picks the mate nearest the ball's *current* position, not the soonest to reach it. It is also skipped entirely while any pass is in flight (`seats_update`, `nearest_to_ball`), so an AI pass to a different mate leaves the human's man on the raw stick for the whole flight. Finally, `meet_choose` accepts meeting points up to 1.9 m high, although feet and body control stop at 1.45 m (`gain_check`, `WP_BAND_BODY_MAX`). It also ignores bounces, though `T_BOUNCE_GRIP` removes up to 39% of horizontal speed per bounce. Both errors push lob landing estimates too long.

The owner's complaint is geometry, and the numbers settle the cone. Control requires the ball's swept path to pass within **1.0 m** (0.8 m against the other team's pass in flight). A player running straight along his stick passes a stationary ball at distance d, θ off line, at d·sin θ. He therefore misses it once θ exceeds asin(1/d): **30° at 2 m, 23.6° at 2.5 m, 19.5° at 3 m**. Against an opponent's pass the 0.8 m reach makes these angles tighter still. An 8-way keyboard (`wp_keymap_read` emits ±127 per axis) is up to **22.5°** off any bearing. A keyboard player therefore cannot reliably run onto a ball 3 m away, even when he presses the best key. A crossing ball adds timing to direction: a 0.15 s error at 8 m/s is 1.2 m, beyond reach. The audit derived these figures by hand and did not measure how often they occur in live play. For contrast, the AI's own chase (`AI_CHASE` in brain.c) steers every tick to `meet_choose` at up to sprint speed, so today the AI already plays a better loose ball than the human.

## A stick-gated chase cone, defined so "off" really means off

The core addition is a new legs-only step, `seats_chase_assist(s, seat, io)`. It runs in `players_step` immediately after `seats_assist_receive` and only when that function left the stick untouched, so the D-020 receive keeps priority. Like every existing assist it rewrites only `mv`, the legs. Buttons, kick aim and first-touch direction keep reading the raw `pin`.

**When it may act.** All of the following must hold. The phase is PLAY. The ball has no owner, because an opponent-owned ball is the tackle's business and EA's defender fix shows why a pull there is wrong. The seat's player is not mid-kick, mid-tackle, mid-header or holding the ball as keeper. The stick magnitude is at least `T_CHASE_STICK_MIN`, so **a neutral stick never chases a loose ball**. No pass is in flight to a *different* team-mate (`pass_target >= 0` on his team and not him), which is YSoccer's "passing mate, let him get the ball" rule. And the ball must be "his", which replaces GRF's designated player. Rather than a binary gate, this is a second continuous weight: full when his earliest (no-preference) meeting time is no later than his fastest outfield team-mate's, fading linearly to zero at `T_CHASE_SHARE_S` later. Comparing like with like matters, because the steering itself uses a stick-preferred point that may be later than his earliest one. A continuous fade also means two near-equal team-mates cannot make the pull flicker on and off, so no state is needed. Without this gate a forward making a run past a ball his midfielder is about to collect would be dragged onto it. `team_plan.c`'s `time_to_ball` is the right helper, but it fills its `tt[]` array only in the not-in-possession, not-reading branch, so those times are not always there to reuse. Call `meet_choose` (no preference) directly for the two or three team-mates nearest the ball instead.

An opponent's pass in flight is also an unowned ball, so the cone is live there too. A defender pointing at the lane gets turned onto the interception point, the stick-gated version of EA's Pass Block Assistance. Against that pass his reach is the tighter 0.8 m (`T_INTERCEPT_REACH`), and it stays that tight. Whether this is desirable or too strong is a playtest question worth asking explicitly.

**Where it steers.** It steers to the meeting point from `meet_choose(s, p, stick_unit, 1)`. With a stick preference, that already picks among reachable points by `t/2 + 0.3·(1 − cos)`. For a crossing ball, the stick therefore chooses between attacking it early and meeting it later on the move, which extends D-020's reception semantics to every loose ball. **The gate angle θ is the smaller of two angles: stick to the meeting point, and stick to the ball's current ground position.** The owner points at the ball he sees, and for a ball rolling across him the two bearings can differ widely. The *rotation*, though, always goes toward the meeting point and is capped (below), so aiming at a fast crossing ball never swings him further than the cap. Chasing needs a variant of the solver, `meet_choose_chase`, not an edit to the shared function. `meet_choose` also drives the AI chase, the team plan and the D-020 receive, and changing it would change the AI and make "Off" no longer the old sim. The variant has three differences. Its height cap is `WP_BAND_BODY_MAX` (1.45 m) instead of 1.9 m, unless a header is wanted. It applies the grass-bounce law. Our reading of `ball.c` is that the bounce update (restitution, linear term, cut-off, grip) is plain arithmetic with no RNG, so it can run on a copy; verify this when implementing. And its `reach_v` comes from the player's *current* mode, `player_top_speed(s, i, sprint_held)`, rather than an assumed sprint. Otherwise, because the assist leaves sprint to the human, a walking player would be steered toward points only a sprinter can reach. Fixing the shared `meet_choose` (height cap and bounces) for the AI and the receiver is worth doing too, but as a separate step with its own golden-hash update.

**How strongly.** Compute the cosine c of θ and the angle weight w = clamp((c − cos E)/(cos F − cos E), 0, 1). F is the full-strength half-angle and E is the edge. Multiply w by the share weight. Then rotate the stick's angle toward the meeting point by **w × min(|Δ|, F)**, where Δ is the signed angle from stick to meeting point. `fx_atan2`, `fx_sin` and `fx_cos` already exist in `fixed.h` on 16-bit `wp_angle`. The legs keep the human's stick magnitude. With the default F = 30° and E = 55°, a stick within 30° of the meeting point is snapped exactly onto it. At 35° the turn is about 25°, and at 50° about 7°. At 45°, the neighbouring keyboard key when the ball lies dead on an axis, it is about 14°. That leaves him roughly 31° off, a likely miss at 2 m, so the neighbour key is honestly "not quite at it" on Normal. High (40°/70°) turns it about 34°, which is enough. **At 55° or beyond the turn is exactly zero, and it can never exceed F.** Because w is continuous and reaches zero at the edge, an analogue stick resting near 55° produces a near-zero pull rather than a toggle. So v1 needs no engage/release hysteresis and no per-seat assist state, and `T_ACCEL`'s 18 m/s² cap already smooths the velocity. The techniques research suggested hysteresis (engage at 45°, release at 60°), but that matters only for a binary engage. Staying stateless keeps the "bit-identical outside the cone" guarantee simple.

**What it does not do.** It never sets the sprint bit. Stamina (D-034) makes sprint a real cost, and the human must choose to spend it. It never changes speed, never adds a reach bonus, and never moves a player whose stick is released. The "ball right there" case is covered by the same cone. Within 1.6 m (`KICK_START_REACH`), a stick within 30° is turned exactly onto the ball, and within 1.0 m the swept `gain_check` takes it on contact.

```c
/* seats.c — legs-only; called only when seats_assist_receive left io untouched */
void seats_chase_assist(const SimState *s, int seat, WpSeatInput *io) {
    int me = s->seat_player[seat];
    if (me < 0 || s->assist_level[seat] == WP_ASSIST_OFF || s->phase != WP_PHASE_PLAY || s->ball.owner >= 0) return;
    /* + busy (kick/tackle/header/keeper hands), |stick| < T_CHASE_STICK_MIN, pass in flight to another mate */
    fv2 st = fv2_norm(stick_vec(io));
    Meet m = meet_choose_chase(s, p, st, sprint_held);       /* pref; z <= 1.45; bounces; reach at current mode */
    if (!m.found || m.t > T_CHASE_MAX_T) return;
    fx c = fx_max(fv2_dot(st, unit(m.pos - p->pos)), fv2_dot(st, unit(s->ball.pos - p->pos)));
    fx w = fx_mul(fx_clamp(fx_div(c - cosE[lv], cosF[lv] - cosE[lv]), 0, FX_ONE),
                  share_weight(s, me));                       /* 1 if first to it .. 0 at +T_CHASE_SHARE_S */
    if (w == 0) return;                                       /* hard zero: raw stick, bit-identical */
    wp_angle a = fx_atan2(st.y, st.x);
    int16_t  dl = (int16_t)(fx_atan2(m.pos.y - p->pos.y, m.pos.x - p->pos.x) - a);
    int32_t  turn = clamp(dl, -angF[lv], angF[lv]);           /* never more than F */
    a = (wp_angle)(a + (int32_t)(((int64_t)turn * w) >> 16)); /* integer x Q16.16 weight */
    write_stick(io, fv2_from_angle(a, FX_ONE), stick_mag(io));/* human's magnitude; buttons untouched */
}
```

The assist level should be a per-seat byte in `WpMatchConfig`, beside `ai_level[2]`, copied into `SimState` as `assist_level[WP_MAX_SEATS]`. It must be hashed in `sim_hash()`, with `WP_SIMSTATE_SIZE_EXPECTED` updated. The pause menu sets it, and a change takes effect on restart, like difficulty does today. Whether it can also change mid-match through `WpInput` is a small extra and **the owner's call**. The golden run drives seat 0 by script for its first three quarters (`tests/test_determinism.c` `advance`), so **the golden hash will change, on purpose**. Do it in two commits. First land the level plumbing with every seat at Off: only the hash of the new byte should differ, while event counts, switches and shot or tackle tallies in `test_determinism` stay identical to today. Then switch the scripted seat to Normal and update the golden deliberately. Cost is one 80-step `meet_choose` per seat per tick, plus the plan times already computed. Against today's 17.7 µs/tick this should be small, but bench it rather than assume.

## Fix the hand-off, latch and switching the cone leaves behind

**AI passes to the human.** `seats_pass_handoff` should stop returning early when the kicker is AI and the receiver is a seat's current player. It should instead set `pass_stick` to the receiving human's own stick at the moment of the kick. That is exactly what an own pass does today, since the passer and receiver share one seat's stick. In `recv_update`, a released stick or one unchanged from that reference is "no opinion". So a human already holding a run when an AI through ball is played keeps the RUN/STRIDE plan, which a `{0,0}` reference would wrongly treat as an opinion. A stick changed after the kick becomes a preference among meeting points, and one held more than 120° away for 250 ms is a run of his own. The same early return also skips a pass between two human seats (a receiver already seat-owned), and the same fix with the receiving seat's stick covers it. This is the smallest change that matches what FIFA ("move toward the ball's destination"), YSoccer and WE all do for a targeted receiver. It does mean a neutral stick walks him to a pass aimed *at him*, which is the existing D-020 behaviour for his own passes. The receive assist forces sprint when the plan needs it (`seats_assist_receive`). Now that stamina exists, whether that should continue for AI passes is worth a one-line question to the owner.

**One-way takeover.** Once `pass_stick` becomes −128, the chase cone itself provides the way back. A receiver who ran away and turns back toward the ball within 55° gets the loose-ball help without reviving the full plan. No change to the takeover rule is needed. **Passes that die below 1 m/s** likewise fall through to the chase cone, so ending the pass no longer strands the receiver. **The latch** should compare sticks with `T_RECV_STICK_TOL` rather than exact inequality, so analogue jitter does not cancel it.

**Switching by time, not distance.** This is where SWOS and YSoccer put all their help, and it is the second half of "the game helps me reach the ball". The loose-ball auto-switch should choose by `meet_choose` time, which `team_plan` already computes, using YSoccer's hysteresis: switch only when the challenger needs less than 0.5× the controlled man's time. It should also stop skipping the switch whenever *any* pass is in flight, and skip it only while a pass targets the seat's own man. Done this way, a long ball is chased by whoever will actually arrive first, and the chase cone then guides that man in.

## What the player keeps, and the tests that prove it

The contract is short. **The human always owns** whether to move (neutral = stop on any loose ball), how fast (stick magnitude), whether to sprint (button only, never set by the chase), every action and its aim (`pin` stays raw), and any direction outside the cone, which is bit-identical to assist Off. **The game owns** only the last 30° of heading toward a ball he is clearly going for and is first to, plus D-020's targeted-receive behaviour. Three choices are genuinely the owner's. Should the targeted-pass assist also apply to AI passes with a neutral stick (recommended yes)? Should the assist be a menu setting with **Normal** as the default (recommended)? And should a released stick ever chase a loose ball, as GRF's designated-player magnet does? We recommend no, since SWOS, YSoccer and the "plays for me" worry all point that way.

The tests belong in a new `tests/test_chase_assist.c`, built on `test_reception.c`'s `scene`/`step2` helpers. Each compares one scenario run twice, at Off and at Normal, which the determinism rules make exact. The percentages are unmeasured starting targets, to be replaced by the first measured baseline.

| Stale-stick release | latch active, stick jitters ±2 units | latch survives (tolerance), cancels on a real change |

A last guard covers the cumulative feel. A 5-minute 7v7 run with a scripted seat that holds noisy 8-way sticks should report the fraction of ticks with w > 0 and the mean correction when engaged. Today's values become the baseline in STATUS, and a test bounds them, failing if a later change makes the assist engage far more often or correct far more than it does now. That is the measurable form of "the game plays for me". The playtest guide should ask the owner to toggle Off/Normal/High in the pause menu on three drills: a loose ball near his feet, a long clearance, and an AI team-mate's pass. Then ask which level felt like his own player.

## Conclusion

The research turns the owner's question around. "Assist toward the ball" is not one feature but three, and WildPitch already has the hardest one, a predicted-meeting-point solver that its AI uses every tick and its human almost never does. The fix is mostly plumbing. Let the existing `meet_choose` serve the human's legs through a bounded cone. Stop discarding AI-made passes. And switch by time instead of distance. Designing the cone so its weight is *exactly* zero beyond 55° does more than look tidy. It is what lets a deterministic test prove that the assist never acts against the player's stick. That is a guarantee we could not find in GRF's blended magnet, and one no commercial game documents.

What remains unknown is feel. No shipped game publishes a cone, a strength or a duration. The 30°/55° default is derived from WildPitch's own 1.0 m reach and the keyboard's 22.5° quantisation, not copied from anywhere. The per-seat assist level, and later a per-player learned strength (Madden already ties its assist to ratings), is how WildPitch will find the right values with its owner rather than guessing them.
