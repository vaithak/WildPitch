# Fix pass_pick's coverage before its ranking

WildPitch's `pass_pick` already uses the same structure as every football game whose code or design we could read: an angular cone around the stick, then one cheap tie-break. Its ranking is mostly right; it picks a wrong team-mate on only about **2.5%** of picks. What it lacks is *coverage* and *legibility*. **37% of keyboard presses pick nobody, and 86% of those untargeted 12 m/s balls leave the pitch.** A 20° keeper cone leaves **22% of keeper bearings unreachable from any key**. Receivers are judged at a predicted point the player cannot see, so pointing an analogue stick straight at a visible team-mate finds him only **87%** of the time. The stick at J-release falls back to a lagging body facing, and nothing on screen shows who the ball will go to. The best practice from HCI target selection (the bubble cursor and IntenSelect), from commercial games (Mario Strikers' movable target, EA's receiver lock) and from analytics (Spearman's receiver model is only about 68% accurate on its own) points one way. Keep the stick dominant. Never leave dead space in the cone without a deliberate, safe "into space" ball. Show the would-be receiver before the commit, with a little hysteresis. Use lane risk only to break near-ties. The proposal below does exactly that in six small playtestable steps, and the only measured gain so far is widening: 62.9% → 70.0% picks. The striker's 0% pick rate on the forward key is caused by `team.c` positioning, not by selection, and belongs to CP0.3.

## Every engine uses a cone plus one tie-break, and WildPitch already does

**The open-source human-pass code converges on one pattern: a hard angular cone around the aim, then "nearest" by some metric, with no opponent check.** SWOS keeps team-mates whose bearing *from the ball* is within ±16/256 (±22.5°) of the 8-way facing. It takes the smallest squared distance, with no prediction and no maximum range, and passes along the facing at a free-pass speed when the cone is empty ([swos-port player.cpp:743-767](https://github.com/zlatkok/swos-port/blob/5acc902cfcb791903ad5077f495986e6e7a32cb6/src/game/player.cpp)). Because facing is quantised to 45° and each cone is ±22.5°, the eight cones tile the circle exactly, so every team-mate belongs to exactly one key. YSoccer searches a 27.5° cone and picks the mate with the fewest frames to reach the ball's predicted path. It then corrects the kick angle toward him by at most ±22.5°, and only if the stick did not move during the 0.15 s kick window ([YSoccer Player.java:1095-1141](https://github.com/ysoccer/ysoccer/blob/478eaef3738e8eef2a7758406663aff237572048/java/core/src/main/java/com/ygames/ysoccer/match/Player.java); [PlayerStateKick.java:22-73](https://github.com/ysoccer/ysoccer/blob/478eaef3738e8eef2a7758406663aff237572048/java/core/src/main/java/com/ygames/ysoccer/match/PlayerStateKick.java)). Code the Classics' *Substitute Soccer* uses `dot > 0.8` (±36.9°) around the 8-way facing, under 300 px, nearest by straight distance. Its source says opponent checks are skipped for humans because "that's the player's job" ([soccer.py:187-218](https://github.com/Wireframe-Magazine/Code-the-Classics/blob/8a2be24283fee68fe65a320dbdf3150ccbab6ee8/soccer-master/soccer.py)). Freekick 3 gives humans no assist at all, just stick × power ([MatchSDLGUI.cpp:958-964](https://github.com/anttisalonen/freekick3/blob/baac95a345b498d14664cb567ba382de680b0d2c/src/match/MatchSDLGUI.cpp)).

**Google Research Football (GRF) is the one soft-scoring system.** It has no cone, no range limit and no keeper exclusion. Every team-mate costs `0.8·(clamp(|pred − aimPoint|, 0, 70)/70)^0.8 + |angle off stick|/π`, where the aim point sits `power·60 m` along the stick. The prediction is `pos + vel·lead`, with the lead capped at **0.7 s**. With no stick, the body facing is the aim ([GRF AIfunctions.cpp:1043-1112](https://github.com/google-research/football/blob/3d9e754720a95621bba6475c4d3b0d56fe919014/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/AIsupport/AIfunctions.cpp); [humancontroller.cpp:617-620](https://github.com/google-research/football/blob/3d9e754720a95621bba6475c4d3b0d56fe919014/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/player/controller/humancontroller.cpp)). By that arithmetic, 30° of angle error costs about as much as 10 m of distance error. GRF also *re-runs* the pick at the touch frame with the then-current stick ([humanoid.cpp:447-476](https://github.com/google-research/football/blob/3d9e754720a95621bba6475c4d3b0d56fe919014/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/player/humanoid/humanoid.cpp)). Every implementation breaks ties by array order through a strict `<`. SWOS writes this down: "Keep the first player on ties" ([swos-port ai.cpp:615-650](https://github.com/zlatkok/swos-port/blob/5acc902cfcb791903ad5077f495986e6e7a32cb6/src/game/ai/ai.cpp)).

**Commercial games publish levers, not formulas.** No vendor has published cone angles or weights. EA's FC 25 patch notes show that power and receiver are coupled and tuned: "Low powered Ground Passes were sometimes being delivered to farther players than intended". They also show that open-ness enters accuracy ("accuracy of short Ground Passes to open players") and that the target leads the receiver (free-kick passes failing "if the receiver was moving too quickly") ([EA FC 25 Gameplay Refresh](https://ea.com/games/ea-sports-fc/fc-25/news/fc-25-pitch-notes-gameplay-refresh-update)). FC 26 picks the semi-assisted through-ball receiver by "the passing lane/gap between two defenders" the stick points through ([FUT.GG summary of the FC 26 deep dive](https://www.fut.gg/news/top-10-gameplay-changes-coming-to-ea-sports-fc-26/)). eFootball's optional Smart Assist goes further and re-targets to "players who are open … if an opponent is blocking a passing lane" ([Konami eFootball overview](https://www.konami.com/efootball/en/page/overview)). That override is unpopular enough to have drawn a petition to remove it ([change.org](https://www.change.org/p/we-demand-that-konami-remove-smart-assist-from-efootball)). A fan eFootball tutorial warns that high assistance misfires "especially if there are 2 player in the same generic direction" ([PES Mastery](https://pesmastery.com/efootball-passing-tutorial/)). SWOS designer Jon Hare says that "passing skill opens up … the width of the passing cone" and that next-player selection follows the joystick direction ([sensiblesoccer.de Q&A](https://www.sensiblesoccer.de/forum/swos-related/24921-i-bring-you-the-word-of-go-ehm-mr-jon-hare-gameplay-mechanics-q-amp-a-session)). Rematch has no receiver selection: the 360° tap goes where the stick points ([Play Rematch](https://www.playrematch.com/post/feature-focus-tap-mechanics)). Mario Strikers lets you hold the pass button and move a target marker between team-mates, and adds separate Free Pass / Free Lob inputs for balls into space ([The Nerd Stash](https://thenerdstash.com/how-to-get-the-free-pass-in-mario-strikers-battle-league/), secondary). Secondary settings guides describe an EA "Pass Receiver Lock" option (Late / Animation Start / Power Up) that decides when the continuously re-evaluated receiver freezes ([FC 25 controller settings guide](https://fifauteam.com/fc-25-controller-settings/), secondary).

**A known commercial failure mode is "the pass went to the farther player".** It comes directly from letting power choose depth along the aim line, and EA patched it in FC 25 (the fix quoted above). Fan forum threads describe the same complaint, but they could not be fetched, so this report does not rely on them. WildPitch deliberately does *not* couple J-hold to the choice of receiver: the hold only sets how deep a through ball runs, per D-020. That avoids EA's main failure mode, and this report recommends keeping it so. No source documents a back-pass or keeper rule in any commercial game. Among the AIs, GRF multiplies a keeper's rating by 0.7 ("don't like playing back to goalie"), Freekick 3 by 0.2, and HELIOS excludes keepers near their own box ([GRF elizacontroller.cpp:862-936](https://github.com/google-research/football/blob/3d9e754720a95621bba6475c4d3b0d56fe919014/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/player/controller/elizacontroller.cpp); [Freekick 3 AIActions.cpp:271-343](https://github.com/anttisalonen/freekick3/blob/baac95a345b498d14664cb567ba382de680b0d2c/src/match/ai/AIActions.cpp); [HELIOS strict_check_pass_generator.cpp:353-428](https://github.com/helios-base/helios-base/blob/0ca18c29a8206c297454b40f4258b09edb98892d/src/player/planner/strict_check_pass_generator.cpp)).

| | Candidate filter | Ranking | Prediction | Empty cone | Opponents |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SWOS | ±22.5° of 8-way facing, measured from the ball | nearest to ball | none | free pass along facing | human: none; CPU: first body in cone must be a team-mate |
| YSoccer | ±27.5° | fewest frames to the ball line | 2 s ball path | raw kick | none |
| Substitute Soccer | ±36.9°, < 300 px | nearest | 8-iteration lead | none | human: none ("player's job") |
| GRF | none | 0.8·dist-to-aim-point^0.8 + angle/π | ≤ 0.7 s lead | n/a | human: none |
| EA FC / eFootball | cone + power (inferred) | power picks depth | yes | manual/precision: to a point | assisted/Smart Assist: yes |
| **WildPitch** | 55°, 2-26 m; keeper only if no outfield mate, ≤ 20° | 10·deg + 8·m (+16/m past 18 m) | d/12 s, uncapped | untargeted 12 m/s along stick | none |

On structure, then, WildPitch matches the field: a soft score in GRF's spirit inside a cone like CtC's, with an explicit range limit that GRF lacks. Where it falls short is everything around the score: what happens at the cone's edge, which stick direction is read, and whether the player can see the choice before committing to it.

## HCI and analytics agree: always one best target, shown before commit

**Pointing research says fixed zones fail at both ends.** Grossman and Balakrishnan's bubble cursor resizes its hotspot so that "there is always exactly one target inside." Its activation regions are the targets' Voronoi cells, and it beat point cursors on both speed and errors (1.58% vs 2.98%). Their analysis of fixed area cursors is exactly the WildPitch symptom: too large and several targets are captured, too small and there is empty space with no target ([Grossman & Balakrishnan, CHI 2005](https://www.dgp.toronto.edu/papers/tgrossman_CHI2005.pdf)). IntenSelect puts a scoring function inside a cone and accumulates scores over time with "stickiness" and "snappiness" to avoid rapid switching between targets. It shows the current winner by bending the selection ray toward it ([IntenSelect, EGVE 2005](https://diglib.eg.org/items/54676737-8fc7-4188-9131-73a8b6e16118/full); [Frontiers in VR 2021 comparison](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/frvir.2021.684677/full)). Shooter aim assist uses nested angular zones per weapon. It traces the spine, not a flailing hand, so momentary motion doesn't drag the assist, and it draws a debug cross where the shot will land ([Witchfire aim assist](https://www.theastronauts.com/2019/02/witchfire-aim-assist)). Its strength is a live-tuned number: The Finals cut controller magnetism from 50% to 35% ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/the-finals-drops-major-nerf-on-controller-aim-assist-targets-controller-spoofing-on-pc)). Three lessons carry over to a pass picker. Selection should be stable over time, judged from a steady body point, and visible before the commit.

**One HCI inference fails WildPitch's own test.** The HCI notes suggest a flat ±22.5° angle tolerance for 8-way keys, on the grounds that the intended angle is uniform within one key step. The audit actually measured that "dead-band" variant offline, and it *lowered* nearest-key reachability from 92.2% to 89.7%. Inside the dead-band, distance decides, so the nearer of two mates in one octant hides the other from his natural key ([audit §3](<../research_notes/Pass receiver selection/wildpitch_audit.md>)). The measurement wins over the inference. WildPitch's steep angle term already behaves like an angular Voronoi partition with distance as a tie-break: one 45° key step costs 450 points, the same as 56 m. The bubble cursor's real lesson for this game is the *no-dead-space* rule, and stage B and the safe free ball below address that. The steep angle gradient is not the problem. A hard outer limit should stay, unlike in a pure bubble cursor, because passing into space is a legitimate intent in football. Rematch, Mario's Free Pass and FC's Precision Pass all provide it.

**Football analytics cannot replace the stick, but it supplies the lane primitive.** Spearman's physics model predicts the receiver as the team-mate most likely to control the ball first, and is right only **67.9%** of the time ([xG Football Club summary of Spearman 2017](https://thexgfootballclub.substack.com/p/the-perfect-pass-formula-can-physics), secondary). SoccerMap's pass-success surface uses positions, velocities, distances and angles, and even a single logistic unit reaches log-loss 0.384 against SoccerMap's 0.217 ([Fernández & Bornn, arXiv 2010.10202](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.10202)). Opta's xPass uses pass angle, distance, start location and context features ([The Analyst](https://theanalyst.com/articles/expected-pass-completion-explained)). If rich models get the receiver right only about two times in three, a game that picks "the best pass" over the player's direction will feel like eFootball's Smart Assist. Analytics belongs *inside* the directional neighbourhood as a tie-breaker, and in the CP0.3 AI. Its real-time primitive is the time-to-intercept race. In the Friends-of-Tracking pitch-control code, a player keeps his velocity for a reaction time, then runs at vmax: TTI = t_react + ‖target − (p + v·t_react)‖/vmax, softened by a logistic with σ = 0.45 s ([Metrica_PitchControl.py](https://www.github.com/Friends-of-Tracking-Data-FoTD/LaurieOnTracking/blob/master/Metrica_PitchControl.py)). GRF's AI uses a cheaper per-opponent version: `danger += clamp(ballTime − (perp + 1)/sprint + 0.5, 0, 1)`, and odds = 1 − danger ([GRF elizacontroller.cpp:1094-1157](https://github.com/google-research/football/blob/3d9e754720a95621bba6475c4d3b0d56fe919014/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/player/controller/elizacontroller.cpp)). That version is all multiplies and clamps, so it is trivial in Q16.16.

## Thirty-seven percent of presses find nobody, and most of those balls leave the pitch

The audit swept 42,624 keyboard presses per configuration with the real 5v5 and `team.c`. It ran every press forward on a copy of the state ([audit §2](<../research_notes/Pass receiver selection/wildpitch_audit.md>)). Four caveats apply to every number below. The opposition is passive, so interception rates are a lower bound. The team-mate geometry is the CP0.2 placeholder. Samples are autocorrelated. Gamepads arrive only in CP0.4, so analogue figures are harness-only for now. H1 is opposition on with a 60-tick settle; H2 is a 300-tick settle and the cleaner baseline.

**Coverage dominates every other fault.** 62.9% of presses pick a receiver, 1.0% pick the keeper, and **37.1% pick nobody**. Untargeted taps then go out of play **86%** of the time (13,092 of 15,225). That is structural: a 12 m/s ball rolls about 18 m, the pitch half-width is 12.5 m, and two-thirds of the start points sat at y = ±8. In **8.3%** of all presses nobody was picked even though an outfield mate stood 2-26 m away within 70°. Those are exactly the presses a 55-70° second stage would rescue. Forward-component keys pick nobody **58.8%** of the time (H1). Most of that is geometry, not selection. `team.c` puts the striker 7 m ahead of the ball and the midfielder 2.5 m *behind* it (team.c:81-82). So the striker on the ball has **0%** picks on the forward key and 15-20% on the forward diagonals, and no selection rule can find a man who isn't there.

**The keeper cone has a hole.** The keeper is tried only when no outfield mate qualified, and only within 20°. Since keys are 45° apart, bearings 20-25° from both neighbouring keys are dead. That is **22% of keeper bearings**, and a constructed scene confirms the keeper is unreachable from any key at a 24.1° offset. The defender's back-diagonal pick rate is only 46%.

**Judging at the predicted point costs what the player can see.** The angle is measured to `pos + vel·d0/12`. That prediction is uncapped: 2.2 s for a 26 m mate, against GRF's 0.7 s cap. It also uses a different flight model from `pass_solve`. A stick pointed at a mate's *predicted* bearing finds him 97.2% of the time, but pointed at his *visible* bearing only **87.2%** (H2). In one constructed case a mate 4 m away running across at 6 m/s is picked by a stick at his visible bearing, while key R, 21.8° off that bearing, picks somebody else, because his predicted point has swung to about 43°.

**Stability and stick source are second-order but visible.** The pick for a held key differs from the pick 12 ticks earlier (one tap) on **4.5%** of samples. With no stick, `facing` lags behind cuts, so the no-stick pick disagrees with the held-key pick on **8.9-9.4%** of the 15 ticks after a cut. Nothing previews the receiver: neither `src/game/` nor any HUD reads `kick.to`.

**Ranking and lanes are smaller problems than they look.** A clearly nearer mate who is at most 15° worse in angle gets passed over in **2.3-2.6%** of picks. Forward keys pick a man behind the passer in **0.6%**. **22.3%** of picks have an opponent within 1 m of the lane, and **28.9%** of those are intercepted, against 0.36% of the rest. But only **10%** of blocked picks had an open alternative in the cone, because with four team-mates most keys have a single candidate. A lane penalty therefore changes just 1% of picks, measured offline. It is worth having for near-ties, for the preview and for the AI, but it is not a cure.

**Verdict against best practice:**

|---|---|---|---|

| Dead space | Bubble cursor: none; games: deliberate free ball | 37% of presses, 86% of those out | **Behind** |
| Keeper / back-pass | AI: keeper ×0.7/×0.2; human: undocumented | 20° keeper cone, 22% dead band | **Flawed** |

## The proposed pick: two stages, two bearings, one remembered stick

The pick keeps the current score and adds structure around it. Every constant is tagged **[S]** if source-backed, **[A]** if audit-measured, or **[D]** if it is a design value to tune in playtest.

**Effective direction.** The direction comes from `pin[i]`, the stick the player actually uses. If the stick is non-zero, use it. A keyboard diagonal released one key at a time must not collapse to a cardinal at the kick, so a **diagonal latch** applies. If the stick is now a cardinal that was part of a diagonal held within the last `T_PICK_DIAG_LATCH` = 4 ticks (67 ms) [D], use the diagonal instead. This is fighting-game input buffering applied to passing. If the stick is zero, use the last non-zero effective direction if it is at most `T_PICK_STICK_MEM` = 30 ticks old (0.5 s) [D]. That follows SWOS's "keep the last pressed direction". Fall back to `facing` only after that. The latch and memory shape only the pass direction, never movement. The memory resets whenever the seat's player changes.

**Bearing.** For each outfield team-mate j, compute the predicted point `qp = clamp(pos + vel·d0/T_PICK_FLIGHT_V)` exactly as today, and use it for the range filter and the distance term. Measure the angle twice, to the *visible* position and to `qp`, and use **off = min(off_now, off_pred)**. Pointing at the man or at where he is running then both work. The audit's two analogue numbers (87.2% visible, 97.2% predicted) bound what this should achieve. Capping the lead like GRF would also help, but it would move the 26 m range filter. The min-of-two rule fixes the visible-bearing failure without touching range.

**Stage A, unchanged in spirit.** Candidates are outfield team-mates with 2 m ≤ dist ≤ 26 m and off ≤ `T_PASS_CONE_DEG` = 55°. The score in integer points:

```c
int32_t pts = (int32_t)(((int64_t)off * 360 * T_PASS_W_ANGLE) >> 16)     /* off in wp_angle units (65536/turn) */
            + (int32_t)(((int64_t)dist * T_PASS_W_DIST) >> 16);
if (dist > T_PASS_PENALTY_M) pts += (int32_t)(((int64_t)(dist - T_PASS_PENALTY_M) * T_PASS_W_FAR) >> 16);
pts += (int32_t)(((int64_t)fx_max(0, T_LANE_CLEAR - clear) * T_PASS_W_LANE_M) >> 16);  /* step 5 */
if (j == prev_preview) pts -= T_PASS_STICKY;                             /* step 4; hysteresis */
/* winner = lexicographic min of (pts, j) in step 0 (= today's behaviour); (pts, off, j) from step 1 */
```

The weights stay at 10 points/° and 8 points/m (+16/m beyond 18 m) [A: baseline], so that 1 m ≈ 0.8°. The code comment saying the reverse is wrong and should be fixed. The audit found mis-ranking rare (2.3-2.6%) and the dead-band variant harmful, so re-weighting is not justified on today's evidence. A smaller angle weight stays an untested idea.

**Stage B, only if stage A is empty.** For *digital* input (both axes ∈ {−127, 0, 127}), add outfield mates with 55° < off ≤ `T_PASS_CONE_WIDE_DEG` = 70° [A: 67.5° measured +7.1 points of picks; 70 rounds up to cover the "within 70°" no-pick bucket]. Stage A fills first, so **adding stage B changes no stage-A pick** (given the bearing rule above, which does re-score moving mates on its own and must be measured separately). The measured plain 67.5° cone swapped about 0.8% of picks that already had a receiver, and the two-stage form avoids that by construction. Stage B also guards against going backward: when the effective stick has a component along the attack axis, a stage-B candidate must not lie more than `T_PASS_WIDE_BACK_M` = 1 m [D] on the opposite side of the passer along that axis. On UR, for example, that blocks a man 115° from goal-ward. The keeper enters in stage B too, with `T_PASS_KEEPER_CONE_DEG` = 25° [A: > 22.5° closes the dead band]. He is scored with the same formula, so a keeper dead-ahead of L at 10° (100 points + distance) beats an outfielder 60° off (600 + distance), as a player pressing L would expect. The keeper is never a candidate in stage A. That keeps the existing "no firm back-pass by accident" rule. Analogue input skips the wide outfield band, because a precise stick pointed at empty space means space, but it keeps the keeper band.

**Which kinds use what.** `pass_pick` serves four callers: tap, through ball, lob (all in `make_kick`) and the header pass (`header.c:95`).

| Kind | Stage A | Stage B outfield 55-70° | Keeper (stage B, 25°) | Preview + hysteresis | Nobody fits |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tap (J ≤ 12 ticks) | yes | digital only | yes | yes | safe free ball |
| Through ball (J held) | yes | digital only | **no** (D-020 plans every runner toward goal) | yes | long free ball (D-020 12→20 m/s, capped at the line) |
| Lob (L) | yes | digital only | **no** | reads `pick_recv` unless it is the keeper (then a stateless pick without him) | as today |
| Header pass | yes | digital only | yes | no (stateless) | as today (8 m cushion) |

**When nobody fits: a safer untargeted ball, not a nearest-mate fallback.** Every game with an explicit "nobody there" path sends the ball into space: SWOS's free pass, Rematch, Mario's Free Pass, FC's Precision Pass. Snapping to a mate more than 70° off the key would recreate the "illogical recipient" complaint. The fix is the ball's *weight*. Today an untargeted tap leaves at 12 m/s plus half the passer's run velocity (`INHERIT_VEL`) and leaves the pitch 86% of the time. Instead, at the *contact* tick (where `contact()` sets `b->vel` today, player.c ~293-296, so the passer's last steps count), cast the kick direction from the ball to a rectangle inset by `T_FREE_MARGIN_X` = 2.5 m and `T_FREE_MARGIN_Y` = 1.5 m [D]. Let L_in be the distance to where the ray leaves that rectangle. If the ball starts outside it, as with a carrier on the touchline, use the far-side exit crossing; if the ray never enters it, L_in = 0. Set the roll distance D_tap = clamp(L_in, `T_FREE_MIN_ROLL` = 6 m, 18 m) [D; 18 m = today's 12 m/s roll]. Then the ball velocity = dir · `ball_roll_speed_for(D)`, *with no inherited run velocity*. That matches targeted passes, which D-020 already plays without it. The roll-table inverse exists, so this costs one lookup. With the inheritance in place, a sideways run would push the ball across the margin and the stop-inside guarantee would fail. A ball still leaves when L_in < 6 m (pressed straight at a nearby line), which is the honest consequence of that input. The through-ball hold with nobody in the cone keeps D-020's meaning, "hold = harder and deeper". Let L_line be the distance to the *actual* line, and D_max = max(D_tap, min(L_line, R(`T_THROUGH_FREE_MAX`))), where R(20 m/s) ≈ 31 m. The hold's roll is D = lerp(D_tap, D_max, h). That is monotone in the hold (a longer hold is never a shorter ball, even when the line is near), capped at the current 20 m/s, and reaches the line at most instead of rolling 10 m past it. This amends D-017 ("12 m/s + inherited run") and D-020 ("12→20 m/s"). The cost of going out rises in CP0.4, when kick-ins replace today's "placed back inside" rule, so this matters more over time.

**Lane danger: a capped tie-break and a preview tint.** Start from the form the audit measured, not from GRF's time race. GRF's constants saturate on a 40 × 25 m pitch: a 10 m pass with an opponent 3 m off mid-lane already scores 0.42 danger, and only a 6 m gap scores zero. The audit, by contrast, places the real risk inside 1 m (28.9% intercepted there, 0.36% beyond). For each candidate's segment from ball to `qp`, and for each opponent, let u be the projection parameter, skipping anyone outside 0 ≤ u ≤ 1. Advance the opponent by `vel·min(u·dist/T_PICK_FLIGHT_V, T_LANE_LOOK)`, with `T_LANE_LOOK` = 0.3 s [D]. Then clear = his perpendicular distance to the segment, and the lane's clearance is the minimum over opponents. Penalty = `T_PASS_W_LANE_M` · max(0, `T_LANE_CLEAR` − clear), with 100 points/m and 1.5 m [A: the measured variant]. The tint uses danger = clamp((1.5 m − clear)/1.5 m, 0, 1). With at most 4 candidates and 5 opponents that is 20 projections a tick. The penalty tops out at 150 points, about 15° or 19 m, so it can reorder near-ties but cannot override the player's key. That keeps the semi-assisted contract and avoids Smart Assist's. Offline this changed 1% of picks [A]. The constants are uncalibrated against active opponents; recalibrate them against the audit's lane buckets once CP0.3 defenders press. Keep the term, because the CP0.3 AI needs this function and will add the time race on top.

**Preview and hysteresis: the sim owns it, the HUD draws it.** Add per-seat fields to `SimState` with a distinct `pick_` prefix, to avoid confusion with the existing `pass_stick` and `latch_stick`: `int8_t pick_recv[WP_MAX_SEATS]` (receiver index or −1), `uint8_t pick_danger[WP_MAX_SEATS]` (0-255, for a tint), `int8_t pick_stick[WP_MAX_SEATS][2]` with `uint8_t pick_stick_age[WP_MAX_SEATS]`, and `int8_t pick_diag[WP_MAX_SEATS][2]` with `uint8_t pick_diag_age[WP_MAX_SEATS]`. Hash every field in `sim_hash()`, update `WP_SIMSTATE_SIZE_EXPECTED`, and initialise `pick_recv` to −1. Each tick in `players_step`, call a new `pass_pick_update(s, seat, i, &pin[i])` before `handle_buttons` for every seat player. It updates the stick memory first, then recomputes the pick *if* the phase is PLAY and the player owns the ball or it is within `KICK_START_REACH`. Otherwise it sets `pick_recv = −1`. Hysteresis subtracts `T_PASS_STICKY` = 40 points [D: 4° or 5 m, an order of magnitude below a 450-point key step] from the previous receiver's score while he stays eligible, in the IntenSelect spirit. A key change still switches immediately; jitter at a boundary does not. At release, `make_kick` reads `pick_recv[seat]` for taps, through balls and (unless he is the keeper) lobs. That is the value computed this same tick from the same stick, so **the stored preview always equals the outcome**. The ring on screen is at most one tick older; the hysteresis keeps the shown man unless a rival beats him by 40 points on the release tick itself, which in practice means a key change on that very tick. If the preview is the keeper and the kind is a through ball, it falls back to a stateless pick without him. The preview has to show on every owned tick, not just while J is held: a tap lasts at most 200 ms, too short to read. `render.c` draws a thin ring under `pick_recv`'s feet, next to the existing seat triangle and power bar. The ring can be tinted toward amber by `pick_danger`, and can be hidden by `hide_markers`. Optionally, a small chevron at the free ball's planned stop point covers the −1 case. Presentation only reads; nothing writes back.

**What belongs to CP0.3, not to selection.** The striker's 0% on the forward key and the 4-18% forward pick rate near the opponent goal come from `team.c` placing the striker on the far side and clamping his target at x = 18.5. They also come from the midfielder parking behind the ball, which already broke one-twos. CP0.3's support logic needs a "show ahead of the carrier" candidate action, so that the striker on the ball has a forward or diagonal option. The AI's own pass choice should follow the split every AI codebase uses. Value comes first: forward progress and space, GRF-style `tacticalRating` with a role mindset, keeper ×0.7. Feasibility comes second, and either GRF's `_GetPassingOdds` time race or HELIOS's harder "any opponent reach-tick ≤ receiver's → drop" can supply it ([HELIOS strict_check_pass_generator.cpp:1245-1310](https://github.com/helios-base/helios-base/blob/0ca18c29a8206c297454b40f4258b09edb98892d/src/player/planner/strict_check_pass_generator.cpp)). SWOS's one-liner, "the first body in the cone must be ours", is the cheapest sanity filter for that AI. ## Conclusion

Asking whether `pass_pick` "picks the right man" turned out to be the wrong question. When there is a man in the cone, it usually does. The failures players will feel come from what happens at the cone's edge (dead space, a keeper you cannot reach, a ball that rolls out) and from a choice they cannot see coming. That changes where effort should go. A second cone stage and a weighted free ball fix coverage without disturbing any pick the first stage already makes. The two-bearing angle is the one deliberate re-ranking, and it moves only moving targets, toward where the player is actually pointing. The sim-owned preview turns the remaining near-ties from surprises into something the player can correct before release, which is the bubble cursor's lesson applied to football.

The research also argues for restraint in two places. Coupling hold to receiver depth, EA's lever and a failure EA had to patch, should stay out. So should letting lane safety override the key, eFootball's Smart Assist and its petition. The lane function still gets built, but its main customer is the CP0.3 AI, and the forward-pass gap in the audit has to be solved there, by giving the striker somebody to pass to.
