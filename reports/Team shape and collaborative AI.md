# Give the team a plan before players choose

WildPitch's players swarm the ball because only one of their candidate actions, SHAPE, is anchored to the player's role, and that action cannot win. Every other off-ball candidate is placed relative to the ball or to an opponent. SHAPE's utility tops out at 1.0, while SUPPORT routinely scores 2 to 4. The measured result is about **4 of 6 outfielders within 10 m of the ball in possession, an 11 m-long team on a 55 m pitch, and SHAPE winning 0-16% of decisions** ([audit](../research_notes/Team%20shape%20and%20collaborative%20AI/wildpitch_audit.md)). Every system whose internals we could read solves this the same way. Google Research Football (GRF), HELIOS, B-Human, Buckland's Simple Soccer and SWOS all combine **one designated ball-winner chosen by time-to-ball with hysteresis, a hard cap on extra pressers and markers, and a ball-dependent formation slot for everyone else**, then let local scoring refine around that slot. The fix for WildPitch's instinct brain fits D-008/D-023 and has three parts. First, add a small per-tick **team plan** in `SimState`: the ball-winner, one cover, up to two zone-limited markers, rest defenders, one near-support and one designated runner. Second, add a **ball-indexed formation table** with separate in- and out-of-possession shapes, interpolated in Q16.16. Third, generate support candidates **around the player's own slot**, and add two features: F_ROLE, which replaces F_FIRST, and F_HOME, a leash to the slot. The plan's targets come from real 7v7 tracking scaled to WildPitch's pitch. In possession the team should be about **17-20 m long, 21-25 m wide and 190-250 m²**. Out of possession it should be about **13-17 × 17-21 m and 130-180 m²**. It needs **two or more rest defenders**, **four or more outfielders goal-side** when defending, and real line gaps of 5-10 m. Build it in seven small steps, a harness step followed by six playtested ones, each with a check that runs in CI.

## Ball-relative candidates and an uncompetitive anchor create the blob

The audit shows that the swarm is not literal chasing. Out of possession, exactly one player runs CHASE/PRESS 79% of the time, and three or more do so only 2.5% of the time. The crowd comes from the 5-10 m ring around the ball. In possession, SUPPORT targets are generated only on a 7-12 m ring around the ball (7 angles × 2 radii). The mean SUPPORT target is **9.0 m from the ball and 16.1 m from the player's own home spot**. Out of possession, MARK candidates exist for every opponent, for every player, with no zone limit, so WildPitch's defenders mark the opposing *defenders* 40% of the time. The team therefore shadows the opponents around halfway, and only **1.97 of 6 outfielders are goal-side of the ball**. Defenders stand more than 1 m ahead of the ball 57% of the time ([audit](../research_notes/Team%20shape%20and%20collaborative%20AI/wildpitch_audit.md); [brain.c `gen_off_ball`](../src/sim/brain.c)).

The weights make this self-reinforcing. SHAPE scores `1.0 − 0.15·d_home`. A typical open support spot scores about 2.1. A halfway mark beats SHAPE whenever `d_home > 1.8 m + 0.8·d_mark`. Players are actually 12-18 m from their spot on average, so SHAPE scores about −1.3 to −1.7 and almost any rival beats it. Once a player is a few metres out of position, he never returns. The anchor is also weak by design. `home_spot` shifts every line by the same `0.55·ball_x` and `0.3·ball_y`, with only +4/−1 m for possession. As a result, even perfect adherence yields a 15.4 m-long, 221 m² team that cannot stretch in possession or squeeze out of it ([brain.c `home_spot`](../src/sim/brain.c); [sim.c `formation_frac`](../src/sim/sim.c)). Two more defects compound this. `ball_rank` uses Euclidean distance to the ball predicted 0.4 s ahead, and each player recomputes it on his own staggered beat. As a result, **45% of PRESS samples come from a player who is not the first man**. There is also no team-level concept of rest defence: **41% of in-possession samples have nobody 5 m or more behind the ball**.

The ablations show the causal chain and rule out the cheap fixes ([audit Q5](../research_notes/Team%20shape%20and%20collaborative%20AI/wildpitch_audit.md)).

| Ablation (team 0, 10 × 5 min) | IN length / hull | OUT length / hull | Goal-side OUT | Defenders ahead of ball OUT | ≥3 in CHASE/PRESS OUT |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline | 11.1 m / 97 m² | 10.9 m / 89 m² | 1.97 | 57% | 2.5% |
| No MARK candidates | 11.2 / 114 | 11.7 / 98 | 2.27 | 56% | **54.5%** |
| SUPPORT ring centred between ball and home | 14.4 / 164 | 14.5 / 146 | 2.37 | 45% | 0.8% |
| SHAPE bias 1.0 → 3.0 only | 20.4 / 235 | 22.3 / **260** | 3.34 | 20% | 0.1% |

The table shows three lessons. First, deleting MARK uncovers a worse failure: with MARK gone, 54.5% of the time three or more players chase, which is literally "everyone to the ball". The press cap must therefore land *before* marking is restricted. Second, raising SHAPE's bias doubles the team's size, but the team becomes **larger out of possession than in it** (260 vs 235 m²). That is the opposite of real football. The second defender within 8 m of the carrier also falls from 79% to 52%. One anchor with one shape cannot produce both a compact defensive block and an expanded attack, nor a deliberate cover man. Third, moving the support ring toward the home spot helps width and area substantially without raising chasing, which supports generating support candidates around the slot.

## Every working system caps the ball and anchors everyone else to a slot

GRF's built-in AI is the most detailed open reference. Each team designates one possession player, the closest to the predicted ball. A challenger takes over only if `(candidateTime + 10 ms)/(designatedTime + 10 ms) < 0.85`, that is, only if he is at least 15% faster. A hard `huntingPlayersNum = 2` caps how many players chase the opponent carrier, and only within a role-dependent hunt distance. Man-marking is greedy over just the `numMarkedOpponents = 3` most dangerous opponents ([match.cpp](https://github.com/google-research/football/blob/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/match.cpp); [elizacontroller.cpp](https://github.com/google-research/football/blob/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/player/controller/elizacontroller.cpp)). Everyone else targets a formation slot mapped into a ball-following block whose depth and width blend between offensive and defensive values. GRF's defaults are offense depth factor 0.9 vs defense 0.75, and width 0.9 vs 0.8. The blend weight is a continuous, fading `possessionBias` rather than a binary switch. Each role carries a mindset value, from CB 0 through CM 0.5 to CF 1.0, which weights defensive pull and attacking runs ([teamAIcontroller.cpp](https://github.com/google-research/football/blob/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/teamAIcontroller.cpp); [AIfunctions.cpp](https://github.com/google-research/football/blob/master/third_party/gfootball_engine/src/onthepitch/AIsupport/AIfunctions.cpp)). GRF also swaps slots dynamically. It runs an integer-cost Hungarian assignment that minimises the maximum distance to a slot, but only players within about 15-20 m of the action use their swapped slot. Players far from the ball jog rather than sprint ("laziness"), which reads as holding shape.

HELIOS, a RoboCup 2D champion, uses the same idea as a lookup. Hand-placed sample ball positions (115 in `normal-formation.conf`) each store all eleven target positions. Positions in between are interpolated inside the Delaunay triangle that contains the ball, and mirror pairs are stored explicitly. A 2007 comparison found that Delaunay plus linear interpolation performed best among the models tested ([Akiyama et al., JACIII 2007](https://www.fujipress.jp/jaciii/jc/jacii001100080998/); [helios-base config](https://github.com/helios-base/helios-base/blob/master/src/formations-dt/normal-formation.conf); [librcsc `formation_dt.cpp`](https://github.com/helios-base/librcsc/blob/master/rcsc/formation/formation_dt.cpp)). A HELIOS player intercepts only if he is the fastest by predicted steps, or within 3 steps of the ball. Otherwise he goes to his home position, with an arrival tolerance of 10% of the ball distance (minimum 1 m), so distant players stop fidgeting ([bhv_basic_move.cpp](https://github.com/helios-base/helios-base/blob/master/src/player/bhv_basic_move.cpp)). SWOS shipped the discrete version decades earlier. A tactic file is **35 ball zones × 10 outfielders, one byte each (350 bytes)**, and attacking runs, such as the winger arriving in the goalmouth when the ball is in the corner, are baked into the table ([SWOS United hex analysis](https://www.swosunited.com/forum/editing-programming/2136-tactic-file-hex-analysis)). Buckland's Simple Soccer chases with only the closest team member. It swaps home-region tables between Attacking and Defending team states, and it scores a grid of support spots (pass safety +2, shot +1, distance-from-controller +2). Its one "supporting attacker" is whichever attacker is closest to the best spot ([Buckland mirror](https://github.com/wangchen/Programming-Game-AI-by-Example-src/tree/master/Buckland_Chapter4-SimpleSoccer)). B-Human positions non-ball robots by maximising a product of [0,1] ratings that is zero outside each role's region, with a rating that actively repels them from the ball ([B-Human docs](https://docs.b-human.de/master/behavior/position-role/)). The commercial 3v3 game Steel Circus does the same local refinement. Its bots sample nearby points and step to one that is "a bit more advantageous" under an influence-map score: keep pass lines clear, don't stand too close to team-mates ([Seifried, AI in Steel Circus](https://philippseifried.com/blog/2019/12/ai-in-steel-circus-part-1/)).

Commercial games expose the same structure as settings. In EA FC 25, **formation defines defensive shape and Roles define attacking deviation**, and players return to formation slots when possession is lost. A single Defensive Approach value sets line height and how the line treats runners ([EA FC 25 FC IQ](https://www.ea.com/games/ea-sports-fc/fc-25/news/pitch-notes-fc-25-fc-iq-deep-dive)). FC 26 CPU teams choose between "press the player, stay back, or use double contain" ([EA FC 26](https://www.ea.com/en/games/ea-sports-fc/fc-26/news/pitch-notes-fc26-gameplay-deep-dive)). FC 27 makes AI defenders "play more of a supporting role through their on-pitch positioning" and mark the carrier from a slightly longer distance ([EA FC 27](https://www.ea.com/games/ea-sports-fc/fc-27/news/pitch-notes-fc27-gameplay-deep-dive)). The PES 2019 manual makes one presser the default and treats "pressure in numbers" (2-3 players) as an explicit option that "can potentially lead to large spaces opening up". Its Fluid Formation keeps separate kick-off, in-possession and out-of-possession shapes ([Konami manual](https://dds.konami.com/games/manual/pes2019/PC/en/control_team.html); [Game Plan](https://dds.konami.com/games/manual/pes2019/PC/en/plan.html)). Football Manager's FM26 also moved to separate in- and out-of-possession formations ([FootballGPT](https://footballgpt.co/guides/master-fm26-tactics-new-ipoop-roles-pressing-explained); this is a community guide, not SI documentation). The convergence is the point. **Two shapes, one engager plus at most one helper by default, and roles that modulate slot adherence** is now the industry pattern, and it maps directly onto team-plan roles plus a formation table.

## Real 7v7 tracking sets the numbers: expand about 30% with the ball, keep two at rest

The most directly usable data is González-Rodenas et al. (2024). It tracked 7-a-side U19 teams in a 2-3-1 (WildPitch's formation) on a 65 × 50 m pitch. **Attacking** teams measured a median **32.8 m wide, 21.0 m long and 395 m²**. **Defending** teams measured **26.2 m wide, 20.1 m long and 306 m²**. Width contracts about 20% on losing the ball, area about 23%, and length barely changes ([González-Rodenas et al. 2024](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12127938/fullTextXML)). Shape scales with pitch dimensions ([Frencken et al. 2013](https://research.hanze.nl/en/publications/size-matters-pitch-dimensions-constrain-interactive-team-behaviou/)). Scaling by WildPitch's ratios (55/65 = 0.85 in length, 35/50 = 0.70 in width) gives **about 18 × 23 m and 234 m² in possession, and about 17 × 18 m and 181 m² out of possession**. I use this direct 7v7 scaling rather than the audit's scaled 11v11 figures (about 20 × 22 m). The two roughly agree, but the 7v7 data matches the format, the formation and the in/out split. One caveat lowers the out-of-possession length target slightly. WildPitch is denser than the study pitch, at 137 m² per player against 232. Forcher et al. also found that compactness *near the ball* predicts regains while whole-team compactness does not ([Forcher et al.](https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1360861403644016128)). So the defensive block can be a little shorter than the scaled 17 m, as long as the players nearest the ball are tight. Separately, Folgado et al. found that younger teams bunch into long, narrow shapes, while older teams are wider ([Folgado et al. 2012](https://research.hanze.nl/en/publications/length-width-and-centroid-distance-as-measures-of-teams-tactical-/)). WildPitch's 11 × 16 m blob therefore reads as "under-9s", which is the owner's complaint in numbers.

Pressing roles follow the pressure-cover-balance model. The first defender closes the carrier and shows him away from goal. The second defender stands goal-side behind him at an angle, about 45° by coaching convention, close enough to step in if the first is beaten. Everyone else keeps balance away from the ball, and roles rotate as the ball moves ([Soccer Parenting](https://www.soccerparenting.com/blog/principles-defense-explanation-youth-soccer-parents/); [FIFA Training Centre](https://www.fifatrainingcentre.com/en/practice/talent-coach-programme/zonal-defending/developing-defensive-coordination-in-small-groups.php)). No authoritative cover distance exists, so it stays a tunable. For rest defence, Bundesliga tracking finds **3.70 defenders against 2.01 attackers** (+1.69) in a band about 7 m deep and 28 m wide while the team is in possession. Experts agree that a **+1 majority over the opponent's counter-attackers** is the core rule ([Forcher et al. 2023, JSSM](https://www.jssm.org/volume22/iss4/cap/jssm-22-707.pdf)). Defensive transitions also go better with four or more players behind the ball when it is lost (Bauer & Anzer 2021, cited in the same paper). Against a 2-3-1 with one striker, a +1 majority means **two rest defenders** in WildPitch, and one in 5v5. The "5-second" counter-press is a coaching convention credited to Guardiola's Barcelona, not an empirically derived optimum ([arXiv 2307.04215](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.04215)). I scale it down to about 2.5 s for a pitch a quarter the size and make it a tunable. Commonly quoted figures for 11v11 inter-line gaps (about 10 m between defence and midfield) are search-snippet level only. I therefore derive WildPitch's line-gap targets from the formation table below, not from them.

## Redesign the brain in three layers: plan, shape and candidates

### Layer 1: a team plan computed once per tick in SimState

Add `team_plan_update(SimState *s)` in a new `src/sim/team_plan.c` and call it in `players_step` right after `seats_update`, before the per-player loop ([player.c](../src/sim/player.c)). It computes both teams from the same pre-loop state, so the update order cannot matter. Players read the plan only when they generate candidates on their decision beat, so the staggered beat and the difficulty cadence (`T_AI_DECIDE_BY_LEVEL`) still give them human-like lag. Proposed state, which must be hashed in `sim_hash()` and requires bumping `WP_SIMSTATE_SIZE_EXPECTED`:

```c
typedef struct {
    fv2     focus;                     /* ball reference for the shape: EMA of ball pos (tau ~0.25 s) */
    fx      poss_fade;                 /* 0 = out-of-possession shape .. 1 = in-possession shape */
    uint16_t trans_ticks;              /* ticks since our possession state last changed */
    int8_t  last_poss;                 /* -1 loose, 0 them, 1 us (as of last tick) */
    int8_t  winner, cover, near_sup;   /* absolute player index or -1 */
    uint8_t role[WP_MAX_PER_SIDE];     /* per slot: PR_ZONE / PR_WIN / PR_COVER / PR_MARK / PR_REST / PR_SUPPORT / PR_RUN */
    uint16_t sup_timer;                /* ticks until near-support / runner may be re-chosen */
    int8_t  mark[WP_MAX_PER_SIDE];     /* per slot: opponent index this slot marks, -1 */
} WpTeamPlan;                          /* SimState: WpTeamPlan plan[2]; reset in match_place_kickoff */
```

The **ball-winner** is the outfielder with the lowest time to the ball. On a loose ball or a pass in flight that is `meet_choose(...).t`. Against a carrier it is the time to the goal-side press point, which is distance over `T_SPRINT_SPEED`. A challenger replaces the incumbent only if `t_c + 0.1 s < 0.85·(t_inc + 0.1 s)`, GRF's rule converted to seconds. Ties break by slot `k` and then by keeping the incumbent, never by global index. Slot order is identical for both teams and in the mirrored state, so this stays mirror-fair. Two refinements matter. First, while an opponent's pass is inside `LV_PASS_REACT[level]`, the defending team's winner is frozen, so the plan cannot "see" the pass before its players could. Second, a seated human counts as the winner only if he is fastest *and* within 4 m of the ball or moving toward it (velocity dot direction-to-ball > 0). In that case the AI supplies the cover, which is EA's "you engage, one AI contains". Otherwise the AI picks its own winner, so a passive human never leaves the carrier free. `ball_rank` then retires; the header code uses `plan.winner == i` instead.

The **cover** is the remaining outfielder with the lowest time to a point 5 m goal-side of the ball, offset 2 m toward the pitch centre line, using the same hysteresis. **Markers** are capped at 2 (3 when the ball is within 15 m of our goal; 1 in 5v5). Opponents are taken greedily by threat, with ties broken by slot. Each is assigned to the unassigned non-winner, non-cover AI player whose *shape spot* is nearest, but only if that spot is within 9 m of the opponent. A centre-back therefore never follows a forward to halfway, and the zone hands the opponent over instead. **Rest defenders** apply in possession: `n_rest = clamp(opp_counter + 1, 1, 3)`, where `opp_counter` counts opponent outfielders more than 5 m nearer our goal than the ball. The players with the deepest shape spots fill these roles, excluding the carrier. **Near-support** is the one non-rest player nearest a point 9 m from the carrier toward the open side, re-chosen at most every 1.5 s, as GRF re-chooses its forward support player. **Runner** (PR_RUN) is at most one wide midfielder at a time, the one with the clearest lane beyond the last defender, re-chosen on the same 1.5 s timer. The striker is always allowed to run. **Transitions** are handled by `trans_ticks` and `poss_fade`. On losing the ball there is a counter-press window, `T_PLAN_COUNTERPRESS` ≈ 150 ticks (2.5 s). During the window, the cover is offered PRESS as well as COVER (both with F_ROLE = 1), so winner and cover both press, which is the GRF cap of 2 hunters. A third player may join only through the general 0.5 s escape described in layer 3. When the window expires, or once the ball is 8 m or more past the winner toward our goal, the cover reverts to COVER and everyone else drops into the out-of-possession shape. `poss_fade` moves toward 1 in possession at 1/60 per tick (1 s) and toward 0 at 1/30 per tick (0.5 s), because defensive recovery should be faster than expansion. It holds its value on a loose ball, so the shape does not flicker in 50-50s.

None of this is a decision state machine. The plan is a shared *observation*: it gates which candidates exist and sets one feature, but the action is still chosen by argmax over candidate × utility. The learned brain can therefore still re-rank the same set (D-008/D-023). The hard caps (who may PRESS or CHASE, how many may MARK) are deliberate structure, like GRF's `huntingPlayersNum`. Record this choice as a new DECISIONS entry.

### Layer 2: a ball-indexed formation table, interpolated in Q16.16

Move `formation_frac` into a new `src/sim/formation.c` and add `fv2 shape_spot(const SimState *s, int team, int k)`. The function reads `plan[team].focus` and `poss_fade`, interpolates bilinearly over a **5 × 3 grid of ball positions** (x ∈ {−hl, −hl/2, 0, hl/2, hl}, y ∈ {−hw, 0, +hw}) for both the in- and out-of-possession tables, and lerps between them by `poss_fade`. Entries are slot positions as fractions of hl and hw, in attack terms, so one table serves both teams and scales from 5v5 to 7v7. The table stores only the y ≥ 0 rows. For y < 0 the code reads the |y| row, negates y and swaps mirror pairs (k1↔k2, k3↔k5), as HELIOS stores explicit pairs. Mirror symmetry is therefore guaranteed by construction rather than by tuning, provided the y = 0 row is itself pair-symmetric (k1 is the reflection of k2, k3 of k5, and k4 and k6 have v = 0). A unit test should enforce that, or interpolation jumps as the ball crosses the centre line. The size is 2 phases × 5 × 2 × 6 slots × 2 coordinates = 240 entries, a const table outside `SimState` that a drill could later edit, as with SWOS. Fill its defaults from a parametric block and then hand-tune individual nodes, for example tucking the far-side wide midfielder to v = −0.7 when the ball is on the touchline out of possession, which a single parametric formula cannot express.

| Parameter (attack-term metres, 7v7 55 × 35 m) | In possession | Out of possession |
|---|---|---|
| Back line x_b as a function of ball x (bx) | clamp(bx − 12, −20, +8) | clamp(bx − 10, −22, +2) |
| Block length L (back line to striker) | 18 m | 15 m |
| Block width W | 24 m | 19 m |
| Lateral centre y_c as a function of ball y | 0.35·by, clamp ±4 m | 0.6·by, clamp ±7 m |
| Slot depth u (0 = back line, 1 = front): k1, k2 / k3, k5 / k4 / k6 | 0 / 0.6 / 0.45 / 1.0 | same |
| Slot lateral v (fraction of W/2): k1, k2 / k3, k5 / k4 / k6 | ±0.4 / ±1 / 0 / 0 | same |
| Front clamp | ≤ hl − 4 m | ≤ hl − 4 m |

A hand check with the ball at the centre spot confirms that this hits the targets. In possession, the defenders sit at (−12, ±4.8), the wide midfielders at (−1.2, ±12), the CM at (−3.9, 0) and the striker at (+6, 0). That shape is **18 m long, 24 m wide, 268 m²**, with nominal line gaps of **9.9 m and 8.1 m**. Both wide midfielders are inside 6 m of a touchline, two players are 12 m behind the ball and one is 6 m ahead of it. Out of possession, the defenders sit at (−10, ±3.8), the wide midfielders at (−1, ±9.5), the CM at (−3.25, 0) and the striker at (+5, 0). That shape is **15 m long, 19 m wide, 177 m²**, with **5 of 6 goal-side of the ball**. Measured play will run 10-20% smaller than these nominal shapes, because the winner, cover and support players leave their slots, which is why the measured targets below are lower. The 5v5 table uses the same parametric model with a 2-1-1 on 40 × 25 m. It must land in the same step as the 7v7 table, because `run_format(0, …)` runs at every checkpoint. 5v5 uses a marker cap of 1 and one rest defender.

Each slot also carries a constant **role mindset** m, following GRF's per-role values. The 7v7 values are k1, k2 = 0, k4 = 0.25, k3, k5 = 0.5 and k6 = 1.0; in 5v5 the defenders are 0, the midfielder 0.5 and the striker 1.0. Mindset sets three things. The **leash multiplier** (1.5 − m) scales F_HOME, giving 1.5 for defenders, 1.25 for the CM, 1.0 for the wide midfielders and 0.5 for the striker, so defenders are the hardest to drag out of position. **RUN eligibility** requires m ≥ 0.5. **Rest-defence priority** fills rest roles in ascending m, then by deepest spot. Because the multiplier is folded into the feature value (F_HOME = metres × leash), the utility stays linear in the weights.

### Layer 3: candidates around the slot, with the anchor in every utility

Change the feature vector minimally. **F_FIRST becomes F_ROLE**, which is 1 when this candidate is the job the plan gave me: PRESS/CHASE for the winner (and PRESS for the cover during the counter-press window), COVER for the cover, MARK on my assigned opponent, SUPPORT on the ball ring for the near-support, RUN for the striker or the designated runner, and REST for a rest defender. **Add F_HOME**, the distance in metres from the candidate's target to my `shape_spot`, times my leash multiplier. F_HOME is set to 0 whenever F_ROLE = 1, because the plan has already applied the zone. That keeps an assigned marker or the winner from being pulled back by his own leash, and the plan drops a marking assignment once the opponent leaves the 9 m zone (with 2 m of hysteresis), which hands him over. NF goes from 8 to 9. This is the cheapest moment to change the learned brain's input, because no learned brain exists yet.

Candidate generation in `gen_off_ball` changes as follows. CHASE and PRESS are generated only for the plan winner and, during the counter-press window, the cover. The one escape is that anyone whose time to the ball is under 0.5 s also gets CHASE, which is HELIOS's "within 3 steps" rule. COVER is generated only for the cover. MARK is generated for the assigned opponent, plus an unassigned MARK (F_ROLE = 0) for any opponent within 6 m of my spot. SUPPORT becomes 8 points at 3 m and 6 m around my *shape spot*, plus a touchline point for the wide slots. Only the near-support keeps 3 points on the 9 m ring around the ball. RUN is generated only for the striker and the one designated runner. A new **AI_REST** action targets a point at least 8 m behind the ball and within 5 m goal-side of the nearest counter-attacker, matching the 5 m nearest-defender distance in the JSSM data. To keep this left/right-fair, generate ring points in mirrored ± pairs. Break exact ties toward the side of my slot (the sign of v), then toward the ball's side. The audit's flank asymmetry (left 30% vs right 43%) is a reason to add a y-reflection check (below), not to trust the generator. All of these are untested starting tunables.

| Action | Bias | COST | F_HOME (0 when F_ROLE = 1) | F_ROLE | Other features (unchanged) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SHAPE | 1.0 → **1.5** | −0.15 → **−0.05** | (target is the spot) | 0 | CROWD −1.0 |
| SUPPORT | 0.6 | −0.20 (cost = metres to me; the old d_home/2 term goes) | **−0.25** | **+2.5** | FWD 0.10, OPEN 0.35, LANE 0.45, CROWD −1.5 |
| RUN | 0.2 | −0.10 | **−0.15** (striker only) | +1.0 | FWD 0.15, OPEN 0.25, LANE 0.30, THREAT 1.0, CROWD −1.5 |
| CHASE / PRESS | 0.5 | −0.10 | 0 | 4.0 | (F_ROLE replaces F_FIRST) |
| COVER | 0.5 | −0.10 | 0 | 2.4 | CROWD −1.0 |
| MARK | 0.4 | −0.12 | **−0.25** (unassigned only) | **+2.0** | THREAT 2.0, CROWD −2.0 |
| REST (new) | 1.5 | −0.05 | 0 (always a role) | **+2.0** | |

Hand arithmetic, for a wide midfielder (leash 1.0) unless stated, checks that the anchor now wins exactly where it should. Take a player **15 m from his spot**, the audit's typical case, who has no plan role. SHAPE scores 1.5 − 0.05·15 = **0.75**. The best thing he is now offered instead is a slot-ring SUPPORT point 3 m from his spot, which is about 15 m from him. With open 5 and lane 3 it scores 0.6 + 1.75 + 1.35 − 3.0 − 0.75 = **−0.05**. SHAPE wins by 0.8. He has no ball-ring candidate at all, so his old SUPPORT action and target cannot reappear, and its +0.8 commitment bonus cannot fire. As he closes to about 6 m from his spot, SHAPE rises to 1.2 and the same slot-ring point scores 3.7 − 1.2 − 0.75 = **1.75**. He therefore peels off into local space near his slot rather than running onto the exact spot. Now take a player **at his spot**. SHAPE scores 1.5. A slot-ring point 4 m away with fwd +2, open 5 and lane 3 scores 0.6 + 0.2 + 1.75 + 1.35 − 0.8 − 1.0 = **2.1**, so he steps into local space, which is the B-Human/Steel Circus "refine around the slot" behaviour. A centre-back (leash 1.5) gets 1.6 for the same point, barely above SHAPE, so defenders move only for clearly better spots. The designated runner, with a target 13 m ahead, fwd 13, open 8 and lane 4, scores 0.2 + 1.95 + 2.0 + 1.2 − 1.4 + 1.0 = **4.95** and goes. The other wide midfielder has no RUN candidate, which keeps the midfield-to-striker gap. Without that gating his RUN would score about 2.5 against SHAPE's 1.5 even with a leash, and both wide players would bomb on. Out of possession, an unassigned halfway MARK 4 m from a centre-back's spot (threat 0.17) scores 0.4 + 0.34 − 0.48 − 1.5 = **−1.24** against SHAPE's 1.5, which kills the man-to-man drift. An assigned mark 3 m away scores 0.4 + 1.0 − 0.36 + 2.0 = **3.04**, so he marks. The winner pressing from 10 m scores 4.5 − 1.0 = **3.5**, comfortably above everything. Also adopt HELIOS's arrival tolerance of max(0.4 m, 0.1 × ball distance) and GRF's jogging for SHAPE moves more than 20 m from the ball. Both cut churn (currently 1.22 action changes per player per second) and suit the "runs cool" rule.

## Conclusion

The audit's most useful finding is that "everyone to the ball" is not mainly a pressing bug. It is an anchoring bug hidden behind marking, which is why removing MARK makes things worse and raising SHAPE's bias makes the defence *bigger* than the attack. The redesign therefore moves coordination out of individual utilities and into a shared, hashed team plan plus a two-phase shape table. This is the step every reference system took. The weights then only have to say "prefer my job, and stay leashed to my slot", which a linear utility can express and a learned brain can later adjust without being able to recreate the swarm.

The design also turns the formation into something the game can teach. The shape table is 240 numbers indexed by ball position, the same kind of object SWOS shipped in 350 bytes. A drill can therefore change where a player stands as easily as how he scores a pass. Plan parameters such as the hunter cap, counter-press window, marker cap and line height are natural difficulty and team-style knobs. They are what EA, Konami and Sports Interactive expose as sliders, and they give the planned player-learning loop a legible "tactical maturity" axis: young teams bunch, good teams spread. The main open risks are balance (the goal rate shifted under the ablations, and pass completion was never measured there) and how a less ball-centric AI feels next to a human. Both are why the plan ships as six playtested steps after the harness, not as one rewrite.
